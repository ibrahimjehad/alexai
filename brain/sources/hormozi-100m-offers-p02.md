Section 2 (Pricing), chapters 3-5. Hormozi argues "grow or die" and reduces growth to three levers (more customers, higher purchase value, more purchases), defines gross profit and lifetime gross profit, and shows how commoditized offers force price-driven purchases while a Grand Slam Offer creates a category of one, illustrated by an agency moving to a pay-for-performance offer (22.4x more cash upfront). He then covers market selection: the starving crowd, four market indicators (pain, purchasing power, easy to target, growing), health/wealth/relationships, the market > offer > persuasion hierarchy, committing to a niche, and how niching raises price. Finally he makes the case for premium pricing: the price-to-value discrepancy, why copying competitor prices fails, the virtuous cycle of price, and the Gym Launch premium-pricing story.
