Combining two audiences (warm, cold) with two ways to communicate (one-to-one, one-to-many) gives the only four ways to advertise: warm outreach, posting content, cold outreach and paid ads.

## The 2x2
| | One-to-one (private) | One-to-many (public) |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm** (gave permission) | Warm outreach | Post free content |
| **Cold** (no permission) | Cold outreach | Run paid ads |

- **One-to-one / private:** one person gets the message at a time (call, email, DM).
- **One-to-many / public:** many people get it at once (social posts, billboards, podcasts).
- **Automation does not change the category.** Emailing a 10,000-person list is still one-to-one, just done fast by a machine. Automation is one way to put the Core Four "on steroids".

## Decision rule
If you aren't getting as many leads as you want, you aren't doing the Core Four with enough skill or enough volume. Do them, or get crushed by people who do.

## Recommended order (the book's progression)
Start with warm outreach (cheapest, most reliable, builds skill), add free content (grows the warm audience), then cold outreach (no longer limited by who knows you), then paid ads. Keep the earlier activities running as you add later ones; even masters keep doing the basics. After that come "lead getters" who do the Core Four for you (customers, employees, agencies, affiliates).

## When to use
The master map for any lead-generation plan. Audit which of the four a business does, at what volume and skill, and which to add next.
