Scale paid ads in three phases: set up tracking, accept a learning period of losses, then spend as much as capacity allows once it's profitable.

## Phase 1 — Track money
Before spending a dollar, set up accurate return tracking (hire a consultant, watch tutorials). Without it you're playing a casino for as long as you feel like instead of as long as you can afford.

## Phase 2 — Lose money ("investing in a money-printing machine")
- You pay first, so the account goes down before it goes up. Hormozi has lost money more often than he's made it, but wins pay back all losses and more.
- Lose often but small (shut losers down fast); win rarely but big (pour gas on winners).
- Example: $100 on each of 10 ads = $1,000. Nine lose everything; one returns $500. Down $500 — most quit here. Instead, put $10,000 on the winner and get $50,000 back. You may lose 9 or 99 times before a big win; you must spot winners and 2x/3x/10x down.
- Be disciplined about test budgets (see the testing budget rule).
- Building the machine costs money and can take long: one business took a year to get ads profitable (others in their space were profitable, so it was possible) and made back the year's losses the next month. Measure returns on a long horizon.

## Phase 3 — Print money
If you make back more than you spend, spend as much as you can — the real limit is usually another constraint (capacity). Set budget by reversing from the customers you want or can handle.

## When to use
Planning an ad budget, setting expectations with an owner, or deciding when to scale.
