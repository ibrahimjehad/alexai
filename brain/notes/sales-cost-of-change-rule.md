Every change to a process causes a roughly 20% performance dip while people relearn, so only make changes expected to beat that: about a 40% improvement at 50/50 odds.

**The idea (Hormozi's own observation, not research).** Changing a sales, leadership or onboarding process costs ~20% performance even if the new way is better, and takes four to five months to recover. Trading a guaranteed 20% loss for a hoped-for 5% gain is a bad trade, and owners are usually over-optimistic (expect 40%, get 5). Left alone, people get better at their jobs anyway.

**Rules.**
1. Require a risk-adjusted 20% improvement (e.g., 40% upside you believe has a 50/50 chance) before changing anything.
2. Keep a private "big list of ideas" instead of calling emergency meetings.
3. Let ideas season two to four weeks; if you still think about one a month later, it may have legs.
4. Compare it with the other big ideas already on the list; only act when the team has spare capacity.
5. Accept that "some things stay broken": not every flaw is worth fixing.

**When to use.** Before rolling out a new script, comp plan, CRM or sales process; when a founder keeps disrupting the team.
