Only two kinds of people can tell strangers about your stuff — you and everyone else — so the highest-leverage lead machine gets other people ("lead getters") doing the Core Four for you, and ideally recruiting more lead getters.

## Leverage
Leverage = how much you get for the time you spend getting it (the internet beats handwritten postcards). Don't mistake leverage for speed: someone 1,000x ahead isn't moving 1,000x faster — they're doing different, higher-leverage things.

## Four scenarios
1. **You are the lead getter** — you do the Core Four all day. Work high, leads low, leverage low.
2. **You get a lead getter** — they bring the same leads without you. Work low, leads low, leverage high.
3. **You get lots of lead getters** — you spend your time recruiting them. Work high, leads high, leverage higher.
4. **You get a lead getter who gets lead getters** — you recruit once; their recruiting keeps leads climbing. Work low, leads high, leverage highest. This is the $100M leads machine.

## The four lead getters
1. **Customers** — buy, then refer (referrals).
2. **Employees** — people in your business who get you leads.
3. **Agencies** — businesses whose services get you leads.
4. **Affiliates/partners** — businesses who tell their audiences about you.

## Key points
- Lead getters aren't things you *do*; you use the Core Four to get them. So the Core Four stacks: once to acquire lead getters, again when they get leads for you — and it repeats as lead getters recruit more.
- Lead getters start as leads like anyone else; the difference is they also bring others. Ideally every lead becomes a lead getter.

## When to use
When the owner is maxed out on their own hours, or planning how to scale lead flow beyond what one person can do.
