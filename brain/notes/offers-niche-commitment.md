Pick one normal niche and stay long enough to find a working offer; hopping between niches resets your learning every time.

Hormozi's "niche slap" is for owners who try one offer in one market, fail to hit a million, and conclude the market is bad (switching from dentists to chiropractors, for example). Both are normal multi-billion-dollar markets; the problem was the offer. Every market has unpleasant traits. You will fail until you succeed, and switching audiences makes that take longer.

**Specific avatar.** Gym Launch served only micro-gym owners with about 100 members, a signed lease, at least one employee, focused on weight loss, and turned down everyone else (personal trainers, online coaches). That focus kept product and messaging sharp.

**When to broaden.** Below about $10M/year, niching down almost always makes more money. Only about 1 in 250 businesses (0.4%) reaches $10M/year, and many companies pass $30M serving a single niche. Above that, depending on total addressable market (TAM), expand upmarket, downmarket or into an adjacent market where existing services still deliver value. If you are at $1-3M and think you have capped out, you are probably not executing well enough.

**When to use.** When an owner wants to switch or widen markets after a few failed tests.
