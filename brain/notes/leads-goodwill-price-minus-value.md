Businesses lack referrals because their product isn't as good as they think and because they don't ask; the fix is building goodwill — the gap between value delivered and price paid — by adding value, not cutting price.

## Two reasons for few referrals
1. **The product isn't good enough.** If it were exceptional, people would already know and you'd have more business than you could handle. If direct-to-consumer customers aren't bringing customers, the product has room to improve. Useful question: "Why are my customers too embarrassed to tell everyone about my product?" Most products are okay but unremarkable — not worthy of remark.
2. **They don't ask.**

## Goodwill
- Price is what you charge; value is what they get; the difference is goodwill (economists: customer surplus). Lots of goodwill → word of mouth → referrals.
- Two ways to build it: lower price or raise value. Lowering price is temporary and eventually unprofitable ("any fool can sell something for less" — Rory Sutherland). So the question is how to give more value.

## When to use
When an owner says "everyone loves us, we just need to get the word out" — test that claim against referral rate.
