For affiliates, compare the cost to acquire an affiliate with the gross profit (after payouts) from all the customers that affiliate sends.

## Worked example (widget company)
- Cost to acquire an affiliate (CAC) = $4,000.
- Average affiliate sells $10,000/month for 12 months = $120,000.
- 75% gross margin → cost of goods $30,000 → gross profit $90,000.
- Affiliate payout 40% of gross profit = $36,000.
- Left for you: $120,000 − $30,000 − $36,000 = $54,000.
- Affiliate LTGP:CAC = $54,000 / $4,000 = 13.5:1.
Minimum for a decent business is 3:1; aim for 5:1, 10:1+. If you have numbers like this, just do more.

(Note: the audiobook states the result as "12 and a half to 1," but $54,000 ÷ $4,000 = 13.5.)

## If below 3:1 — three fixes
1. Lower CAC: better ads, offer and sales process for recruiting affiliates.
2. Raise LTGP and lower CAC: get more affiliates to activate with a launch process.
3. Raise LTGP: improve long-term integration.

Affiliates add layers (your customers, the people who get you customers, and with super affiliates the people who get those people) — more complex, but worth it if you can manage it.

## When to use
Evaluating or tuning an affiliate program.
