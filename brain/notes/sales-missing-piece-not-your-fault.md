Prospects arrive with partial solutions; show them each past attempt failed because it lacked one or two of the pieces your offer provides, so it wasn't their fault and this time will be different.

**The idea.** Anyone on the call admitted they have a problem they haven't solved, so they must be missing at least one key; "they cannot have them all or they would not be on the phone." That puts you in an unlosable position unless you lose the frame.

**Steps.**
1. In the pain cycle, collect every past attempt: what, how long, how long ago, how it went.
2. For each, ask why they think it failed. Often they don't know, so walk through it together.
3. Map the gap onto your pillars ([sales-three-pillar-pitch]). Fitness example (Ep 112, a meal-delivery diet): the calorie deficit worked (good), but monthly check-ins were too rare (missing accountability), they never learned to cook (given a fish, not taught to fish: missing skill), and there was no resistance training (no muscle to keep the weight off).
4. Close the loop: "Does that make sense why it didn't work, and why this will work for you?"
5. Pair with proof and a guarantee; in Ep 112 he offers the money back if they don't get the service promised, then asks "What card do you want to use?"

**Short version** (Ep 305). "Working out is great, but you also need nutrition, and if you don't follow the plan you need accountability." "You lost the weight and gained it back because you didn't build muscle."

**When to use.** Overview step of CLOSER and whenever "I've tried this before" or "I'm a special case" comes up. Understanding your product deeply matters here: you're acting as a consultant filling in their puzzle.
