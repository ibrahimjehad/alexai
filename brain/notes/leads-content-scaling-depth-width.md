Two valid ways to scale content: max out one platform before adding the next, or get onto all relevant platforms early and then max them together.

## Depth then width
1. Post on one relevant platform.
2. Post regularly there.
3. Maximise quality and quantity (short form up to ~10 posts a day; long form up to ~5 days a week, like soap operas).
4. Add another platform while keeping the first maximised.
5. Repeat until all relevant platforms are maxed.
- **Pros:** maximises return on what you've learned, rides compounding, fewer resources.
- **Cons:** misses easy new audiences, no early "omnipresence", dangerous dependence on one channel that can change or ban you.

## Width then depth
1. Post on one relevant platform. 2. Post regularly. 3. Add the next relevant platform while keeping the previous one going. 4. Continue until you're on all of them. 5. Maximise all at once.
- **Pros:** broader reach faster, efficient repurposing (one short video formatted for every short-video platform).
- **Cons:** more labour, attention and time; often produces lots of weak content everywhere.

Larger existing businesses can scale faster. Best day to start was the day you were born; second best is today.

## Hormozi's production routine (not a full-time creator)
1. ~1 hour finding topics using the five sources.
2. Twice a month, record ~30 short clips.
3. Same day, record 2-4 long videos unpacking tweets that had the best stories or examples.

## When to use
Planning content capacity. Lean resources: depth then width; established team: width then depth with repurposing.
