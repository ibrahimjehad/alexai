A tanning salon sold a $5, five-day VIP pass, then converted pass holders into $19.99/month memberships; Hormozi later adapted the idea into the decoy offer that saved his gyms.

**The tanning play.** Everyone thinks they can tan in five days; they can, but never as dark as they want, and rushing burns. Staff ask how tan they want to get, then give the "turkey talk": a turkey that takes three hours burns if you double the heat to finish in half the time; a good colour takes 5 to 10 sessions with rest days, so more than five days. Then: "Let's credit your VIP pass toward your first month. Why buy $25 day passes when members get unlimited access for $19.99/month?"

**The gym adaptation (about 2019).** When fitness leads got too expensive, Hormozi's team offered two options: free (one session a week) or an "ultimate" version (unlimited sessions, one-on-one coaching, personalization, and a guarantee of results or repeat free). 70 to 80% took the premium.

**Transcript note.** The premium price is spoken as "3.99"; given the deliverables it is almost certainly $399.

**Lesson.** Know the customer's real timeline to results better than they do, and use it to make the premium the obvious choice.
