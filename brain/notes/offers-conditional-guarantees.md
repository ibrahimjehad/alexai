Conditional guarantees attach terms, ideally the actions that produce success, and can be "better than money back".

Ideal: every customer would qualify, but achieves the result and so never claims. Given the choice, most buyers prefer the promised outcome to a refund.

**Options (what the client gets if they meet the conditions and miss the result):**
1. **Outsized refund**: double or triple their money, or a fixed payment far above the price. For high-margin products, with "consumption" conditions (they must do the work). Usually beats a plain 30-day refund on net conversions and can improve results.
2. **Service guarantee**: you keep working free until they reach X. Removes time from the promise; Hormozi's personal favourite. He says no adviser using it has reported a client claiming it: clients who do everything usually succeed, are close and happy, or you gladly keep going.
3. **Modified service**: another period of service free, generally at least twice the original duration. Finite exposure; a gentler start than (2).
4. **Credit-based**: refund as credit toward any of your services; best in upsells (worst case they apply it to what they already like).
5. **Personal service**: you personally work with them one-on-one until they hit the result. Extremely strong; add tight conditions (respond within 24 hours, use the specified products, prerequisites that make failure unlikely).
6. **Hotel plus airfare**: refund the event plus travel costs if they do not get value; tangible, good for workshops.
7. **Wage payment**: pay their hourly rate if the session is not valuable (verify via tax return / 1,960 working hours; almost nobody claims).
8. **Release of service**: let them out of their contract with no fee; costless if you do not enforce contracts anyway.
9. **Delayed second payment**: no further billing until they hit their first outcome (first 5 pounds, first sale, website live). Focuses your team on activation; best when you know the activation metric that predicts retention.
10. **First outcome**: you cover their ancillary costs (ad spend, etc.) until the first outcome, e.g. "no sale in 14 days and we pay your ad spend until you do".

**When to use.** Higher-ticket, higher-cost or B2B offers where success depends on client actions.
