When a prospect doesn't yet value the result enough, widen the gap between where they are and where they want to be, and stack value "to the point of ridiculousness" until the price is obviously small.

**Steps.**
1. **Ask what they want**, then enhance the picture into an emotional experience. A classic: "If we were talking six months from now, looking back, what would have had to happen for you to be really happy?"
2. **Dig beneath the first answer.** Instead of asking "why?" seven times (it annoys people), use "Tell me more about that": is it affecting how you feel as a parent, a partner, at work?
3. **Make it bigger and permanent.** Sell the long term, not the short win: not "lose 10 pounds" but "at 80 you're still mobile, you kept the weight off through your 30s to 60s, you sleep better, you're nicer to your spouse". Go past their safe goal ("I'd be happy at 150"; "What if we got you to your high-school weight and toned?"). Promise improvement, not maintenance; habits spill into other areas: "how you do one thing is how you do everything."
4. **Raise the stakes.** What would someone pay for one more year of life? For their children to be healthy? Ep 47 and 112 call it "generational health". Remind them why they came: they signed up, booked, drove through traffic and found parking, because it matters.
5. **Write the future story.** "Imagine looking back: 'I can't believe I paid all that money, but that was the day everything changed.' How much is that story worth to you?" Then: "If we could do that, is it worth it?" You are really selling a new identity.
6. **Translate features into what they value.** Nobody signs up for a six-week squat-and-deadlift seminar; plenty sign up for a "big booty boot camp" doing the same lifts.

**Caveat.** Only paint pictures you can deliver.

**When to use.** Question 1 of [sales-two-questions-of-persuasion], especially in consumer and emotional sales where ROI maths doesn't apply.
