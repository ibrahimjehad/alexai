An offer broken into named parts and presented as a stack of bonuses is perceived as more valuable than the same offer presented as one thing.

What you do but never list is invisible to the buyer. Infomercials ("but wait, there's more") anchor the price to one core item (a $38.95 knife) and then add knives, sharpeners, pans and guarantees until passing feels stupid. They would not pay for that airtime if it did not return money.

**Mechanism.** Anchor the price to the core offer. Each added bonus widens the gap between price and value until it "snaps the rubber band" holding the wallet. You raise value instead of cutting price. The bonuses are the components produced in trim and stack, now presented one at a time at the right moment.

(Credit: Hormozi attributes his renewed appreciation for bonuses to Jason Fladlien.)

**When to use.** Whenever presenting an offer: never show it as a single lump; enumerate and value each component.
