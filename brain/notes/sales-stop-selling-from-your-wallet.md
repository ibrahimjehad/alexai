Don't set prices by what you or your friends could afford; imagine every prospect is rich, because many are and they judge value differently.

**The idea.**
- Owners in the bottom half of wealth often price for people like themselves and listen to friends and family who also can't pay. "Why would you listen to people who don't have money on how to get money?"
- Being too cheap can make the promise unbelievable ([sales-example-price-raise-lifted-close-rate]).
- Poorer buyers think in cost ("that's expensive"); richer buyers think in ratio ("for what?"). $20,000 is a steal for a Berkshire class A share or a new Lamborghini.
- Wealthy buyers want three things: fast, easy, guaranteed. They'll pay $100 for something that costs you $10 (scouting, pre-choosing, delivering to the door) while low-budget buyers haggle over $5.
- Price is two-way communication: it signals who it's for. Wealthy buyers shop from high to low; a B2B service at $1,500/month tells a large company "not advanced enough for us". Flash sales and discounts tell the wealthy "this isn't for you."
- You can't sell the same commodity as the cheap competitor at a higher price; be so different the buyer must evaluate you separately ([offers-commodity-vs-category-of-one]).
- "If you sell to rich people long enough, they will make you one of them."

**When to use.** When an owner resists raising prices or adding a premium tier, or when close rates are high (see [sales-close-rate-price-headroom]).
