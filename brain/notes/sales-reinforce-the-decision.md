The sale is not done at the yes: most customers judge a business by the first 24 hours after buying, so make specific promises for that window and keep them early.

**How.**
1. At the close, tell them exactly what happens next: "In the next 24 hours you'll get three things: you'll be introduced to [person], she'll do [X], then [Y]."
2. Keep every promise inside that window, ideally faster than promised, so their impression is "these guys are dialed".
3. Onboard the next day, not next week. In Ep 998, Hormozi tells a coach offering a three-day cancellation window to onboard immediately: once people get value, refunds and cold feet drop sharply. "Give value as fast as you can."

**Ep 305 adds.** If you close for someone else's business, have the owner or coach send a personalised video right after the sale ("Hey Johnny, saw you just signed up, you're meeting Heather tomorrow, a gift is in the mail"), plus a handwritten card. There he puts the judgment window at the first 48 hours (this note's later episodes say 24): buyers decide in that window whether they believe in you long term. Teams go quiet for fear of scaring off the sale; do the opposite and overcommunicate, because "what do I do now?" silence is where sales get lost. He also stresses the sale never ends: re-close at onboarding and at the first check-in.

**When to use.** Every close, and especially when you offer a cooling-off period or guarantee. This adds the 24-hour script and the onboard-to-cut-refunds rule to [leads-faster-customer-wins], which covers the broader ~48-hour first-impression window.
