Before selling, ask about what they have tried, why it matters and what not having it has cost, to temporarily increase their deprivation around the outcome.

**The idea.** Like media deciding the "topic of the election", a sales call is a short window where you can make one problem the most important thing in the prospect's life. They arrive a bit hungry; talking about the bad food they have been eating and how good the meal could be makes them hungrier, so they are more likely to take the offer.

**Questions (the O in CLOSER).**
- What have you done so far to try to make this happen?
- Why is this so important to you?
- What else has happened in the meantime because you haven't had it? What has it cost you?

**Ep 112 / Ep 305 add.** The election analogy is older: putting attention on a topic is what makes it important, and prospects often cope with a painful problem by ignoring it. Ep 112 adds "why did you come today?" questions that remind them of the effort they made: signing up, putting it in the calendar, driving through traffic, finding parking. Ep 305 adds follow-ups per attempt (how long did you do it, how long ago, what else have you tried?) and says to loop until every option is exhausted, which gives you ammunition for the close; then show why each failed ([sales-missing-piece-not-your-fault]). Ep 305: "you need to sit in the pain."

**When to use.** Every sales call, after labelling the problem and before pitching. Don't pitch to someone whose pain you have not surfaced.
