Measure advertising efficiency as lifetime gross profit per customer divided by cost to acquire a customer; below 3:1 businesses struggle to scale, above it they take off.

## Definitions
- **LTGP (lifetime gross profit)** = everything a customer ever spends minus what it costs to deliver it. Example: buys $15 items costing $5 to deliver, 10 times → $150 revenue - $50 cost = $100 LTGP. Use gross profit, not "lifetime value"/revenue, because gross profit is the money that actually pays for acquisition, rent and payroll.
- **CAC** = cost to acquire a customer.
- "Getting 3 to 1" = LTGP:CAC of 3:1. LTGP > CAC = profitable advertising; LTGP < CAC = losing money.

## Threshold
Every portfolio business Hormozi saw struggling to scale had LTGP:CAC under 3:1; once above 3:1 they took off. (An observed pattern, not a law.)

## Two levers
1. Lower CAC — more efficient ads.
2. Raise LTGP — better business model (more per customer). Do both for maximum money.

## Ads or business model? Decision rule
- Competitors in an industry have surprisingly similar CACs; winners differ mainly in how much they make per customer. Owners often blame "bad ads" (high CAC) when the real problem is a bad business model (low LTGP).
- Research your industry's average CAC. If your CAC is within 3x of the industry average → fix the business model (LTGP). If it's above 3x → fix the advertising.
- Cost can only approach zero; profit per customer can go to infinity. Past a point, cutting $100 of CAC is harder than making $100 more per customer — like trying to save your way to a billion.

## When to use
Diagnosing whether to work on ads or offer/monetization; setting scale decisions.
