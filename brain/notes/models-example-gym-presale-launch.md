Hormozi's early gym launches pre-sold an empty building and used the cash to build it out, a money model that a famous marketer called a "level 10 skill set" in a "level 2 opportunity".

**The numbers.**
- Put about $3,000 down on a lease, then run a few days of ads for a free six-week challenge until reaching about 20 leads/day at about $5 per lead.
- About half the leads show for appointments; half of those buy a $600 program, so about 25% of leads become customers.
- About $80 more profit per customer from supplements, so about $680 per customer before the doors open.
- Per lead: $5 in, roughly $170 out (25% x $680), which he summarized as $1 in, $34 out within 48 hours.
- Back end: a few weeks later, buyers can apply their $600 as credit toward a one-year membership. About two-thirds convert, giving a full gym and about $20,000/month in recurring memberships within about 30 days, on $3,000 down.
- Cash from sign-ups paid for ads, equipment, flooring, signage and the rest; a new location every six months with no debt.

**What happened next.** The marketer told him to teach other gym owners instead of running gyms; he closed or sold his six gyms and started Gym Launch (30+ in-person turnarounds, then a licensing model; later about $43M in owner distributions and a sale of 66% at $46.2M).

**Lessons.** Front-end cash covers acquisition and build-out; a credit rollover into continuity creates the recurring base. This is the whole book in miniature.

**When to use.** As a reference model for local or service businesses that can pre-sell before delivering, and as proof that 30-day cash can fund growth without debt.
