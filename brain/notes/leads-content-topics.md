Pick topics from personal experience, the easiest way to say something no one else can, using five time-based categories.

## The five categories
1. **Far past:** big lessons from your life tied to what you sell. "Give them the story without the scar." Example: a friend checks his screen time (3 hours a day on social) and says, "I found you some time." Relatable, and it connects to his business audience.
2. **Recent past:** do things, then talk about what happened. Mine last week's calendar: meetings, conversations, warm outreach. Client stories, testimonials and case studies fit here. Example tweet from a portfolio meeting: if everyone else is doing it, don't. Keep notes and recordings so this is easy.
3. **Present:** capture ideas the moment they occur (pause meetings to note them). Hormozi posts ideas publicly as they happen and makes more on topics that outperform.
4. **Trending:** go where attention already is; add your experience or expertise to what's trending. Good for reaching a broader audience.
5. **Manufactured:** create an experience to make content about (e.g. "I lived on $100 for a month"). Highest cost, highest potential payoff.

## Manufactured vs documenting
Manufactured content has the most upside (top creators engineer maximum reward per unit), but documenting your real life is cheaper, keeps volume high and is more sustainable over a lifetime. Hormozi prefers entrepreneurs post more, more often, in more places.

## When to use
Building a content calendar or recording session; Hormozi spends about an hour finding topics this way before each batch.
