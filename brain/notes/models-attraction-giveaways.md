Advertise a chance to win a big prize in exchange for contact details and other information; after picking a winner, offer everyone else the prize at a discount.

Also called scholarships, sweepstakes or raffles: "enter for a chance to win". Many enter, one wins, the rest qualify for a discount on what they already showed interest in, so leads are pre-qualified for your most expensive product.

**Steps.**
1. **Pick a grand prize** = the thing you want everyone to buy. Give it a stated monetary value to anchor price (if you sell $5,000 of value for $2,000, advertise the $5,000 value).
2. **Pick the promotional offer** (the "partial scholarship"): your core offer enhanced with a discount, a bonus, or a minor change from the grand prize, which ethically justifies a lower price against the grand-prize anchor. Call it whatever fits: scholarship, gift card, dollars off, store credit, voucher.
3. **Collect contact information** with permission to follow up any way you like, plus *eligibility* questions (fit: "Do you own a vet clinic?"; need: "Why should you be picked?") and *qualifying actions* (attend a call or event, make a post, join a group) that spread the giveaway and show intent.
4. **Set a deadline**: 3 to 7 days from the start of promotion. Update entrants daily (email, DM, text, posts) with the countdown, the prize's benefits and social proof; treat it like a mini launch.
5. **Announce the winner publicly**, then privately message everyone else that they qualified for something and ask them to book a call. Reason: their story or answers were so compelling you wanted to give them something (a participation trophy).
6. **Second deadline**: the promotional offer expires in 7 days; repeat the countdown; on the call explain cost vs value using their discount.

**Examples.**
- Dentist: grand prize free invisible braces ($6,000 retail); promo $2,000 braces gift card.
- Dog food: free year of organic food ($1,000); promo $300 gift card usable only with a one-year subscription.
- Service: free one-year package ($5,000); promo $2,000 voucher toward a one-year agreement.
- Consulting: free 16-week turnaround ($12,000); promo $6,000 partial scholarship.

**Rules and pitfalls.**
- Giveaways are heavily regulated (illegal-lottery risk); get legal advice. Someone must actually win; make prizes and qualifications clear; say more than one person can win a prize.
- If nobody bites, the prize was not grand enough. A portfolio company's event-ticket giveaway flopped; a $50,000 equipment bundle from a known gym supplier plus a year of their core product "crushed".
- More work to enter means fewer but more qualified entrants; find the sweet spot.
- Ask "why" questions (why us, why now, why does it matter, what's your goal) to get ammunition for the sales call.
- Have downsells ready: tell callers they qualified for two prizes; if they reject the promo, apply the same percentage discount to another product; in recurring businesses spread the discount over the longest term they accept, then bill at normal rates automatically.

**When to use.** To generate a flood of engaged leads for a high-value core offer, especially with a sales team that can call them.
