Tone has only five controllable parts: keep volume, speed and articulation constant for comprehension, and vary only pauses and pitch (up or down).

**Why.** The same words mean different things depending on emphasis, so a good script fails if said wrong. Vague coaching ("curiosity tone", "sound confident") can't be acted on; these five can.

**Three constants (comprehension).**
1. **Volume** - loud enough to hear easily (matters most in person).
2. **Speed** - roughly 135 to 185 words per minute. Too fast lowers trust; too slow sounds dim. Consistent speed also makes call length predictable; he timed his book-launch pitch at his 185 wpm to know when the buy link would drop.
3. **Articulation** - pronounce every word fully, no mumbling. This also automatically slows down nervous fast talkers.

**Two variables (natural sound and turn-taking).**
4. **Pauses** - a short pause draws attention to a point; a long pause signals it's their turn.
5. **Pitch** - rising pitch (a question) solicits a response; falling pitch makes a statement.

Natural variation comes from knowing the script cold, not from acting ([sales-script-memorization-blackout]).

**Script notation.** Only three marks: ellipsis (...) for a short pause, period for a full stop, question mark for rising pitch.

**The close.** The ask combines a long pause with rising pitch: ask them to buy, then stop talking ([sales-pause-after-the-ask]). The close is where emotions and adrenaline peak, so manage tone most there; fast and loud sounds aggressive.

**Coaching translations.** "Have more confidence" -> "speak louder". "You sound nervous" -> "speak slower". This gives managers concrete language and makes results consistent across reps.

**Ep 305 adds (earlier view).** After moving every script to CLOSER, some reps crushed it and others sold nothing; Jordan Belfort's emphasis on tonality explained the gap. His figures there: the words are about 10% and how you say them about 90% (say "Alex?" three ways and it means three things). Words are the logical influence, tone the emotional one; you need both. Two ways to fix tone: train it (slow, years to unconscious mastery) or trick it, because conviction corrects tone ([sales-build-conviction]). He also suggests dropping to a near-whisper to make a point sound important; that conflicts with this later framework's "keep volume constant" rule, which gets emphasis from pauses and pitch instead.

**When to use.** Writing scripts, training reps, reviewing call recordings.
