Offer a higher one-time price alongside continuity; the bigger the one-time price relative to the monthly rate, the more people choose continuity.

**Hormozi's test data (thousands of gyms), standalone price as a multiple of the monthly membership:**
- 1.33x: about 50% choose continuity.
- 1.66x: about 60%.
- 2x: about 70%.
- 2.33x: about 80%.
- 2.66x: about 90%.

Example with a $199/month membership: one-time options of about $266, $333, $399, $466 and $532 respectively.

**Principle.** People will pay about 33% more to avoid a recurring commitment: at 1.33x, half still buy the one-time option. Exact numbers matter less than the direction: smaller premium, more one-time buyers (more cash now); larger premium, more continuity.

**For more cash up front.** Sell the bonus-only option as a single payment 33% to about 166% above the first month of continuity-plus-bonus (the transcript says "33 to 266%", but the multipliers 1.33x to 2.66x correspond to 33% to 166% more). Add bulk prepay discounts on top.

**Transcript note.** The spoken examples pair the multiples with figures like "$399 standalone offer is $266 a month"; the first figures do not match the stated multiples (they appear to be about 1.5x the second) and likely refer to a table in the print book. The multipliers and the $266 to $532 values are internally consistent with a $199 membership.

**When to use.** Whenever you offer both a one-time and a recurring option; set the ratio based on whether you need cash now or recurring revenue.
