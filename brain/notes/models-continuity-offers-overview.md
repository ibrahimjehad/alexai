Continuity offers give ongoing value for ongoing payments until the customer cancels; they attract more buyers than a big one-time price but collect less cash now, so Hormozi offers them last in the sequence.

**Worked comparison.** Offer a $1,000 product to 100 people: 10 buy, $10,000 now. Offer it as $50/month: 40 buy; kept for 20 months each, that is still $1,000 per customer: $2,000 in month one and $40,000 over time, with 4x as many customers to upsell later.

**Trade-off.** Continuity used as the attraction offer leaves you strapped for cash today. Solution: get cash today from attraction, upsell and downsell offers, then add continuity for a little cash now and lots later. Continuity can still be used anywhere (attract, upsell, downsell, re-engage).

**Fit.** Only for ongoing value. Paying forever for a one-day workshop is silly (paying until covered is a payment plan). Conversely, charging one price, even a big one, for service forever is probably a mistake.

**No standalone memberships.** No successful continuity business Hormozi has seen sells only a membership: recurring commitments to untried things are hard to advertise, so they attract with trials and similar offers, then upsell features and longer prepayments.

**The three offers.** Continuity bonus, continuity discount, waived fee. Two use rewards (Hormozi's preference); the waived fee is the punishment/contract option.

**Pattern he uses.** Profitable attraction offer, then upsells and downsells, then continuity; if accepted, upsell a discounted bulk block of time, after which they auto-renew into continuity.

**Maxim.** "You can shear a sheep for a lifetime, but you can only skin it once."

**When to use.** Stage three of a money model, for any product with ongoing value.
