People buy when the value they get clearly exceeds the price they pay; keep that gap wide by growing value, not by cutting price.

The moment value dips below price, customers stop buying. The easy way to widen the gap is lowering price, but that is usually wrong: the goal of a business is to make money, not to make sales. Price can only fall to zero but can rise without limit. Unless you can cut costs to about one-tenth of competitors', do not compete on price. Dan Kennedy: there is no strategic benefit to being the second-cheapest in a market, but there is to being the most expensive.

**Rule.** Raise price only after you have raised value enough that the buyer still gets a great deal (e.g. $100K of value for $10K, "money at a discount").

**When to use.** Whenever someone proposes a discount to win sales: instead, add value until the current or a higher price feels like a bargain.
