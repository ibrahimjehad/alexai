A car salesman kept cutting the price of the same insurance from $5,000 to $400; the buyer said no every time and began to distrust the car price too.

**What went wrong.** At first the no was about money; by the end it was about trust. Lowering the price of the same thing teaches the customer (and everyone they tell) that every price you quote is negotiable: trading trust for a buck.

**What would have worked.** Offering a *different* (lesser) insurance product for less would likely have kept trust and closed the sale.
