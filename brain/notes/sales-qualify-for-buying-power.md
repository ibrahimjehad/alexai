People with more money are more likely to buy your expensive option, so identify what your highest spenders share, tell the market you only serve them, and judge leads by value, not cost.

**Steps.**
1. Offer ways to pay more (tiers, premium options) so your data shows who spends more.
2. Study the top spenders: what makes them different? (E.g., 40-year-old moms with two-plus kids in certain zip codes; homes worth over $1M.) Ask them what attracted them; they value different things than price shoppers.
3. Put those traits in your front-end marketing and say plainly who you serve ("we only work with businesses above X").
4. Score inbound leads before talking to them: e.g., pull home value from the address (Zillow lookup) and call the highest-value homes first.

**Lead cost vs lead value.** On his book launch, a campaign optimised for leads paid ~$5/lead and those leads were worth ~$20 (4x); one optimised for purchases paid ~$17/lead worth ~$189 (11x). Paying 3.5x more per lead returned 9.5x the value. Volume and cost per lead fall when you qualify hard; revenue rises.

**When to use.** Setting ad targeting and optimisation goals, routing leads to reps, and when close rates are under 30% because of poor-fit prospects.
