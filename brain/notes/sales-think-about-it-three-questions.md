People stall because they fear making a mistake and would rather let life decide for them, so help them confront the decision: name the main concern, then check the only three things that matter.

**Script.**
1. "Totally. What's your main concern? Let's think about it together."
2. If fear surfaces, name it: "What are you most afraid of happening? You taking my money and getting nothing, again? Let me tell you why that won't happen."
3. The three questions:
   - "Do you feel what we do can meet your needs and solve the problem?" (the product)
   - "Do you want to work with us? Do you like our values and what we stand for?" (the company)
   - "Do you have access to the funds, or know someone who does?" (the money)
4. Three yeses: "Well then, let's rock and roll."

**Theory.** Understand why each objection happens rather than memorising lines: price means you didn't articulate value; decision maker usually means an earlier mistake; a stall means they need help making a logical choice. The three questions mirror the product/company trust checks in [sales-seven-components-of-a-closed-deal].

**When to use.** Any "let me think about it", "sleep on it" or vague hesitation after the price.
