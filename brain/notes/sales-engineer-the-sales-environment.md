Don't just get better at selling; shape the physical setting and small moments so the frame "everyone buys and gets results" is obvious.

**Tactics.**
- **Floor-to-ceiling testimonials.** When the walls are covered in results, doubting efficacy becomes unreasonable. If someone questions it, glance around the room: "I think getting results should probably be the least of your concerns."
- **Exit line.** When a prospect who didn't buy leaves while the next arrives, send them off warmly as if they had: "Absolute pleasure, can't wait to help you hit your goals. Let's rock and roll." The next person assumes the last one bought.
- **Recruit clients as endorsers** ([sales-overheard-persuasion]).
- **Fix mismatched details** so every touchpoint fits your frame.

**Proof it's the frame, not the room.** Hormozi sold launches out of empty "vanilla shell" spaces with a card table and two folding chairs, about $100k for a free weight-loss challenge with no gym built. The gym existed only in the prospects' minds.

**Ep 22 adds.** The wall is the last stage of a choreographed path: a customer group where members post wins, then a "mandatory" testimonial page or sizzle video sent after booking, then the waiting room and sales room plastered "like wallpaper" with testimonials and reviews ([sales-pre-appointment-testimonial-page]). Third-party proof can say what you can't say about yourself.

**When to use.** Any in-person sale (home services, brick and mortar, gyms, clinics), and the digital equivalents (proof on pages and in waiting rooms).
