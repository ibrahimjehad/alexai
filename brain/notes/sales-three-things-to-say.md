On a sales call you should only ever say three kinds of things: questions, restatements of what they said, and short pre-packaged stories that break a belief.

**The three.**
1. **Questions** (most of the call). Leading questions that walk them to the conclusion that they should work with you: "Why are you here? What are you struggling with? Where are you trying to go? What have you tried? How's that working? Why does it matter?" In complex, high-level sales the prospect has to reach the conclusion themselves rather than feel told.
2. **Restatements.** Say it back so they know you listened: "So what I'm hearing is this is the main issue, this is what you've tried, and this is why it hasn't worked. Does that sound about right?" People pay therapists hundreds of dollars an hour largely to listen; being understood is why they buy.
3. **Short anecdotal stories.** When it's time to explain how you'd help, don't describe the program; tell a short analogy that breaks the belief in the way ("What if we thought about it like this?") and end with "Does that sound fair?" Examples:
   - Gym owner who wants freedom: "Do you see a world where you outsource the most important part of your business, getting customers, and still have a reliable business? You'll have to learn this today, tomorrow or in ten years." Backup: give a man a fish vs teach him to fish.
   - Weight loss: "What's your favourite show? Do you need motivation to watch it? We'll get you to look forward to your food the same way." Or: "Could you never eat cake again for the rest of your life? No? Then we find foods you like more, so you don't need willpower."

Notice none of them is a declarative statement of fact. Have several stories per belief in case the first doesn't land ([sales-pitch-analogies]).

**Why.** "Technobabble" about modules, programming or software is where engagement drops; call recordings show it the moment a rep talks for five minutes straight. More detail also gives the prospect strings to pull ("I don't like Zoom, can you just call me?"). Ep 305 caps the pitch at about three minutes.

**When to use.** Writing or auditing any script: if it has paragraphs of explanation, convert them into questions, restatements and one-line stories. Pairs with [sales-dont-pitch-until-you-know-yes]. Ep 275 adds that higher-level, complex sales need more knowledgeable reps (Hormozi calls his "business consultants"), while low-ticket transactional sales can be tightly scripted with minimal variation.
