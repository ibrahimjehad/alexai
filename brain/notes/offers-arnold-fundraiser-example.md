At the After-School All-Stars fundraiser at Arnold Schwarzenegger's home, scarcity, urgency and bonuses made identical items sell for many times their normal price.

A major donor (a high-end jewelry and watch dealer) advised the charity CEO: "when demand increases, cut supply". They cut the number of tickets and raised the price from $15,000 to $25,000 (invite list about 100). That alone raised about $1M extra before the event began and brought in better-qualified donors. The night raised about $5.4M from roughly 100 attendees, the charity's best ever. Auction items were one-of-a-kind (miss it and it is gone forever), and Arnold added bonuses when two bidders ran high so both donations could be captured. An item that would fetch $10,000 elsewhere sold for $100,000.

Other levers were also at play (commitment and consistency, status, peer pressure, celebrity, competition), but Hormozi focuses on scarcity, urgency, bonuses and guarantees because they belong to the offer rather than the selling.

Note: the audio says "$5,400 a head", but $5.4M across about 100 people is about $54,000 per head.
