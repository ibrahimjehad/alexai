Referral requests only work when treated like an offer that shows what the customer (and friend) gets for referring — and the referrer's risk must be outweighed by benefits.

## The idea
- Customers can only know to refer if you tell them; most businesses never ask.
- Referring risks the customer's goodwill with their friend, so they refer only when they're confident the friend will have a good experience and the personal benefit outweighs the relationship risk. Goodwill (proof you deliver) lowers the risk; incentives raise the benefit.

## Case studies
- **Dropbox:** free storage to the referrer and the referred friend; went viral, ~39x growth in 15 months.
- **PayPal:** $10 credit to the customer and $10 to the referred friend; helped reach 1M users in two years and 100M six years later.

## Maxim
"I am compensated tomorrow for the value I provide today." Give more than you get, then capitalize on the goodwill by asking.

## When to use
Designing any referral program.
