"If you do X or achieve Y within Z days, you get it free": customers pay up front and earn the money back (cash or store credit) by hitting tracked actions, results or both.

**How it works.**
1. Set a goal for the customer and show them how to reach it.
2. They pay now (a deposit or full price).
3. Choose the qualifying basis:
   - *Results*: they win if they hit the outcome (earn $X/month, get Y customers, lose Z pounds). They bet on themselves.
   - *Actions*: they win if they do what you ask (attend all sessions, log progress, take photos, do homework), whatever the outcome. They bet on following instructions.
   - *Both*: actions plus results. Most people lack the skills to reach goals alone, so a good plan plus accountability gives them a fighting chance.
4. Winners get their money back as cash or store credit, which you roll into a bigger, longer offer (see [models-upsell-rollover]).

**Template examples.**
- B2C: "Free 28-day blueprint: deposit $X, get it all back if you attend all consulting calls, post progress weekly, journal daily in our app, attend your feedback and transformation sessions."
- B2B: "5 customers in 5 days free challenge: deposit $X, get it back if you send 100 messages/day, report stats, attend daily trainings, post homework, attend the day-5 consulting call."
- Physical product: Volvo once offered a free car to anyone who drove their Volvo a million miles (turn it in, do photos and a press release, full original price credited to the next car).

**Why it makes money.**
- Many will not qualify even with realistic criteria.
- Those who do qualify often stay, *if* you have something else to sell; have an upsell ready to apply their winnings to.
- The offer attracts far more customers and gets them results; winners advertise for you. Hormozi says it has generated over $1B in sales and grew his gyms better than any other offer; it was Gym Launch's first Grand Slam Offer.
- "The real money comes from people who succeed with it."

**Numbers and thresholds.**
- Across thousands of gyms, about 10% of customers asked for their money back. If you cannot stomach that, do not run it.
- Only use it if your current refund rate is below 5%; otherwise fix the product first.
- Store credit vs cash back got the same number of customers in his tests, so you may as well offer store credit. To still advertise "free", pair it with an unconditional satisfaction guarantee; it did not materially change refunds.
- Personal rule: refund anyone who asks, entitled or not.

**Best fit.** New customers (steepest possible discount, 100%), current customers (mixes them with new), and lapsed customers (bigger incentive to return). Ideal for things people start and quit: business, skills, weight loss, fitness, beauty regimes, self-care, time management, mental health.

**When to use.** Businesses where results need sustained customer effort and you have a follow-on offer.

**Pitfalls.** No follow-on offer (winners leave with their money); vague or hard-to-track criteria; running it with a weak product.
