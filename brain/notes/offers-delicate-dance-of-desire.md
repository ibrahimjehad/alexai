Desire exists only while it is unsatisfied, so selling slightly less than demand keeps prospects hungry and lets you charge more over time.

Naval Ravikant: desire is a contract with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want. Satisfy it fully and it disappears.

**Fractal demand.** Hormozi finds demand for services follows 80/20: about one-fifth of prospects will pay 5x or more. Workshop example: sell 10 seats at $500 ($5,000) or two one-on-one days at $5,000 ($10,000). The second makes more, costs less to deliver, delivers more value, and leaves eight people with unmet desire who watch the buyers love it. Next time they act faster and pay more, and new prospects join the pool, so you can open three spots and still sell out. Repeating the $500 version sells fewer each time because all demand was used up, until no sale is left.

**"Hormozi law":** the longer you delay the ask, the bigger the ask you can make ("the longer the runway, the bigger the plane").

**Balance.** Zero supply makes no money and people eventually leave feeling rejected (slower than you think); full supply kills the golden goose. Aim for "ravenous", not merely interested, prospects.

**When to use.** When planning launches, pricing tiers or capacity: deliberately keep supply just under demand.
