Between asks, respond to any non-yes by acknowledging it, associating it with buying behaviour, then asking again; this keeps rapport so you can ask more times.

**Why.** The closers who close most are the ones who ask most ([sales-ask-the-most-times]). Asking a lot is only possible without losing rapport, and AAA is the bridge. Hormozi says top closers do it unconsciously; when he retrained his team on it, close rates rose across the board.

**The three As.**
1. **Acknowledge.** Repeat back what they said: "Totally understand you need to think about it. Super reasonable." You don't have to agree. It builds rapport (they feel heard) and buys you seconds to think, instead of firing back an overcome that sounds like a sales line.
2. **Associate.** Link their response to buying or to success, so a step away becomes a step toward. Three types, lightest to strongest:
   - *Generic positive:* "Great question. Makes total sense you'd ask that."
   - *Foil:* someone just like them who said the same thing and bought, or got great results ("You remind me of Janet, who said the same six weeks ago and has lost 30 pounds"). Lets you say hard truths to the foil, not the prospect ([sales-third-party-foil]).
   - *Authority:* "I used to believe that too; can I share what a mentor told me?" If the rep lacks status (an 18-year-old selling 40-year-old owners), borrow the boss's: "Funny, Alex told me something about this this morning. Mind if I share it?"
3. **Ask.** Ask permission to share ("Mind if I share what I told her?"), give the reframe, then ask for the sale again or move to the specific overcome.

**When to use.** Every obstacle after price in a one-on-one sale. Not the same as [leads-aca-framework] (acknowledge, compliment, ask), which is for steering ordinary conversations toward an offer.
