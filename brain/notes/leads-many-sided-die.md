A parable for learning to advertise: everyone rolls a die with an unknown number of sides and one green side; every green turns another side green, so whoever keeps rolling eventually rolls green most of the time.

## The parable (summarized)
Two players get dice — one with 20 sides, one with 200 — with one green side each; they can't see how many sides they have. Rolling green turns a red side green; rolling red changes nothing; the game ends only when you stop, and stopping loses. One player keeps rolling through reds and others' greens; greens become frequent. The other rolls a few reds, complains the first player has an easier die, finally rolls green but complains how long it took, spends more time watching and complaining than rolling, and quits.

## Lessons
- Rolled enough, the die you were given doesn't matter; every die hits its green streak.
- You can't see whether another player is on roll 100 or 100,000, or how good they were at the start.
- "Lucky" players are lucky because they play — and rolled again after red.
- Two guarantees: the more you roll, the better you get; if you quit, you lose. "You cannot lose if you do not quit."

## When to use
Encouraging owners through early failed tests of a new channel.
