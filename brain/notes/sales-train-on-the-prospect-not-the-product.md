Most sales training teaches the product; it should mostly teach the prospect: their thoughts, needs, pains and how those show up on a call.

**Why.** A rep who knows the product perfectly but misnames the prospect's problem loses the sale, because the prospect doesn't feel the offer is for them even when it is. And you can't be convinced your product solves a problem you don't understand, so prospect knowledge also feeds conviction ([sales-belief-continuum]).

**Signs of the gap.** Eight minutes into a call the rep doesn't know the prospect's client count, revenue, profit or biggest struggle, but has told them how big your company is.

**Avatar advantage.** Many great businesses start with a founder who had the pain, solved it and then solved it for others: they understand the prospect intimately. If you or your reps aren't the avatar, make up for it with deliberate prospect training (call reviews, common pains, the words customers use).

**When to use.** Designing onboarding and ongoing training for a sales team, especially when entering a new market.
