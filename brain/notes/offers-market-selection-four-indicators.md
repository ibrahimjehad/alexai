Pick markets that score well on four indicators: massive pain, purchasing power, easy to target, and growing.

1. **Massive pain.** They must desperately need, not merely want, the solution. The degree of pain sets how much you can charge.
2. **Purchasing power.** They must have, or have access to, enough money to pay your price. Counter-example: a great resume service aimed at unemployed people, who are in pain and easy to find but cannot pay.
3. **Easy to target.** You must be able to reach them: associations, mailing lists, social groups, channels they all watch. If finding them is a needle-in-a-haystack problem, even a great offer will not be seen. (Wanting to serve rich doctors is fine; showing the ad to nursing students is not.)
4. **Growing.** A growing market is a tailwind; a shrinking one is a headwind no effort overcomes. Newspapers had pain, money and targetability but were shrinking.

Avoid a market failing any of the four; normal markets are fine.

**When to use.** When choosing or validating a niche: score the candidate market on each indicator before building the offer.
