Generate every reason someone should be interested by running the value elements (what) through the perspectives of people who give status (who) across past, present and future (when).

## The idea
People value something when benefits look big and costs look small; the best ads maximize the former and minimize the latter. A good ad answers the questions the prospect is thinking, at the moment they think them, in their own words (Ogilvy: the customer is not a moron, she's your wife — write to her).

## What — eight key elements (value equation + opposites)
Know how your product delivers each value driver and avoids its opposite ("carrots vs sticks"):
1. **Dream outcome** — show the maximum benefit. Opposite: **nightmare** — the worst pains of going without you.
2. **Perceived likelihood of achievement** — explain away past failures, show success of people like them, authority, guarantees, "at least a better chance than now." Opposite: **risk** — how risky it is to carry on as they are; problems repeat and get worse.
3. **Speed** — how much faster they get it. Opposite: **time delay** — current trajectory is slow or never arrives.
4. **Ease** — avoid what they hate, keep what they love, no hard work or skill needed. Opposite: **effort and sacrifice** — the work and skill needed without you, or that they're already working hard and getting nowhere.

## Who — status
Humans are status-driven, and status comes from how others treat us. List (a) the customer gaining status and (b) those giving it: spouse, kids, parents, family, colleagues, bosses, friends, rivals, competitors. Apply each perspective to each value element for bonus benefits (weight loss: kids get a role model, spouse gets healthy too, promotion; business: spouse won't nag about a risk-free purchase, competitors' phones stop ringing, buddies notice the new car).

## When — timeline
Show consequences across past, present and future, both toward good and away from bad, and from others' perspectives too (teased as a kid / jeans won't button / another belt loop; doctor warns you may not walk your daughter down the aisle). Then contrast with the good future if they buy.

## Combine
Combine all three: who + what + when (e.g. spouse notices how fast you fit into the suit; kids, month after month, get interested in eating healthy). More angles covered = more interest. Long ads simply cover more angles than short ones.

## Rules
- "As specific as you can, but no specific-er": specificity raises efficiency but lengthens copy; too long for the platform lowers efficiency. Use visuals (contrast, likeness, scene) to add specificity without adding length.
- You don't need to be a great copywriter: if you call out the right people and have a great offer, you barely need copy — just explain the offer clearly. Get good enough to be profitable, then scale.

## When to use
Writing the body of ads, landing pages, sales scripts or content; generating many ad angles from one core offer.
