If buyers can compare you like-for-like they buy on price; differentiate the offer so they buy on value instead.

**Commodity.** A product available from many places, so purchases are made on price. If all options look equal, the cheapest is the best value by default. When a prospect thinks "these are basically the same, I'll take the cheaper one", they have commoditized you. Commodities get priced at market efficiency: competition pushes margins down to just enough to keep the lights on.

**Category of one.** A differentiated, value-driven offer that cannot be compared, so the decision is your offer versus nothing. This resets the prospect's "value meter": they assess your offer on its own merits. You do not need to explain why you are different; the offer itself makes it obvious.

Key point: the delivery work can be identical in both cases. Only the offer (promise, terms, bonuses, guarantee, price) changes how it is perceived.

**When to use.** When prospects keep asking for discounts, comparing quotes or switching to cheaper providers: the fix is a differentiated offer, not a lower price.
