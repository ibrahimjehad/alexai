When they say "that's expensive", don't get defensive or discount: agree proudly, explain you chose to be the best, restate what it does, and ask whether they want to keep taking the cheap route.

**The four parts.**
1. **Agree.** "Absolutely, it's definitely expensive. Hopefully we're the most expensive, because we're definitely the best." Pause and let it land.
2. **The decision we made.** "We had a decision to make: compete to be the cheapest, or give people what they actually need, more attention, accountability, coaching, everything others cut corners on, which is why nobody gets results. It sounds like you've already tried the cheap route and it brought you here." Get agreement.
3. **Restate the vacation.** "If this does what I say it does [specific outcomes they named], wouldn't it be worth [price]? If I told you all that and said it was $300, would you even believe me?"
4. **The main question.** "Do you want to keep running in circles with the cheap route, or solve this for good? Add up ten years of cheap things and it'll cost ten times this, plus the emotional trouble." Then: "What do you want to use?"

**Why.** Agreeing knocks the prospect off balance (they expected you to cave), and a high price becomes evidence of quality. "You have to deliver hard so you can sell hard."

**Don't fear the gasp.** A friend who was top rep on a 75-person phone floor (about $1M/month in transactional sales) said their rule was: if you didn't get a gasp at the price, you didn't go high enough; they anchored high so they could downsell. Hormozi doesn't endorse the turn-and-burn style but takes the lesson: start high and embrace the gasp. (Compare [models-upsell-anchor].)

**When to use.** Premium-priced offers, right after the price reveal; pairs with [sales-its-expensive-preframe] before it.
