When customers buy something they get other things free; reframe a discount (or a full price) as free stuff, because free draws far more attention than a percentage off.

**Mechanics.**
- *Reframe only*: three $10 T-shirts = $30, or one shirt for $30 and two free. Same price, but now it is a free offer.
- *Reframe plus discount*: three shirts at $6.67 = $20 (33% off), or one shirt for $20 and two free.

**Three versions of an 18-month service agreement at $1,800** (good, better, best):
- Buy 12 months, get 6 free.
- Buy 6, get 6 free.
- Buy 6, get 12 free (most compelling: most free stuff). Everyone pays the same for the same service.

**Rules.**
1. **Raise prices before giving away free stuff** to preserve profit. Actually raise them (do not fake a list price); since all new customers come in on this offer, change prices for at least a season. Some people will still buy at the higher à la carte price.
2. **More free than paid.** "Buy 2, get 10 free" beats "buy 10, get 2 free"; "buy 1, get 2" beats "buy 2, get 1".
3. **Free items can differ from paid ones.** One $10 shirt plus $20 of free socks. More cheaper items can beat fewer expensive ones: test "buy one shirt, get one free" against "buy one shirt, get three pairs of socks free".
4. **Free beats an equivalent discount.** More people understand the value of free than the value of one shirt: test "buy one for $20, get two free" over 33% off.
5. **Manage the cash.** Prepaid cash must cover delivery for the whole term; budget for it, do not spend it (no house with customers' delivery money). Selling what you cannot deliver is illegal and ruins your reputation.
6. **One-time buyers: make the one purchase as big as possible**, with real value behind it.

**Economics.** Customers buy more and stay longer (if they normally stay 3 months, "buy 2, get 2 free" holds them 4), creating more upsell opportunities and up-front cash. One of Hormozi's service businesses took a year to earn its money month to month; "buy 6, get 6 free" attracted far more customers and was paid up front.

**When to use.** Products people can buy more of or services they can access longer; tourist or one-time-purchase businesses; when you need up-front cash.
