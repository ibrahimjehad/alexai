Risk that the product will not work is the number-one objection to any purchase, so reversing it is the fastest way to make an offer more attractive.

Jason Fladlien reported conversion rising 2-4x from changing only the quality of the guarantee. Experienced marketers spend as much time on the guarantee as on the deliverables. Always state your guarantee boldly with a reason why, even when it is "no guarantee".

**Structure that gives it teeth:** "If you don't get X result in Y time, we will Z." Without the "or what", it is weak.
- Weak: "We'll get you 20 clients, guaranteed."
- Strong: "You'll get 20 clients in your first 30 days, or we refund your fee plus the ad spend you spent with us."

**Four types.**
1. Unconditional: pay, try, refund if unhappy. Strongest, more buyers, more refunds.
2. Conditional: terms attached, often "better than money back"; tie conditions to the actions that produce success.
3. Anti-guarantee: all sales final, with a compelling reason why.
4. Implied: performance-based pay (rev share, profit share, ratchets, triggers).

**Stacking.** Combine them, e.g. a 30-day unconditional refund plus a 90-day conditional triple-your-money-back; or sequential conditional milestones ($10K by day 60, $30K by day 90 if you do 1, 2, 3). This future-paces the result and shifts risk onto you.

**Fit by business.** Broad unconditional guarantees suit lower-ticket B2C (many will not bother claiming). The higher the ticket, the more B2B, and the higher the fulfillment cost, the more you should use specific conditional or anti-guarantees, since you eat both the refund and the delivery cost.

**Cautions.** People who buy only because of a guarantee may not do the work; link conditions to success behaviors. Guarantees enhance an offer but cannot rescue a bad product or sales team; that just produces refunds.

**Build your own.** Identify the buyer's biggest fears, pains and obstacles (time, emotion, outside costs) and reverse them; the more specific and creative, the better. Hormozi's advice for starting out: service-based guarantees or performance partnerships, which effectively make all sales final and commit you to results; move to looser guarantees later for volume.

**When to use.** In every offer.
