The person asking the questions controls the sale, so when you don't know which answer they want, hand the question back, find the intention, and check your answer works before you say it.

**Trap questions.** If you don't know what the right answer is, any answer is wrong: answering lets the prospect judge it against their standard and decide whether to buy. Like a lawyer, never answer a question unless you know the answer they need.

**Moves.**
1. **Hot potato.** "How many calls a week do I get?" -> "How many were you looking for?" If they say once a week and you have that: "Perfect, that's covered. Ready to get started?"
2. **Ask for the intention.** "What specifically were you hoping to ask Alex?" or "Why do you want calls with Alex?" ("Why" is almost always available.)
3. **Pre-confirm.** "If you had someone helping with X, Y and Z, or a tested process for X, Y and Z, would that solve it?" Only once they say yes do you explain, then ask for the sale.
4. **Educate with what you have.** If the intention is accountability: "Makes total sense. Can I tell you the three ways we do accountability? We started doing it your way and it didn't get the outcome, so we built this."

**Principle.** Never disagree with a prospect. Keep asking until they say the right answer; they believe what they say, not what you say.

**When to use.** Any feature or "do I get..." question during a call; especially questions that previously stumped reps (e.g., "how many certifications do your trainers have?" -> "Which one were you looking for?").
