"It costs too much" usually means "it costs too much right now"; spread the same price over time, stepping down from most cash up front to least, and stop as soon as they buy.

**Why it works.** Like a discount, it lowers what they pay in the moment, but they still pay full price over time. Payment plans are a gamble: they win when they add customers who complete payments, and lose when people cancel early, worst of all when someone who would have paid in full takes the plan and then quits.

**The seven steps.**
1. **Reward paying in full rather than punishing paying over time.** Quote the price with the financing cost built in, then offer a discount for prepaying ("It's $15, or $10 if you prepay") instead of adding interest ("$10 now or $15 over time"). Same maths, friendlier, and the higher price anchors.
2. **Third-party options that still pay you now.**
   - Third-party financing (a finance company pays you; customer repays them). Takes setup, worth it.
   - Credit card: "Would you rather I decide your payment terms or you decide?" When they say they would, suggest they use their credit card.
   - Layaway: they pay in any installments over a reasonable time and get the product only when paid in full (most flexible for them, lowest risk for you).
3. **Half now, half later.** "When do you next get paid? Want to put half down today and the rest then?" If not: "What's the most you can put down today?" and the rest on payday. Scheduling off paychecks (often every two weeks) boosts 30-day cash more than monthly payments.
4. **Check they still want it.** "On a scale of 1 to 10, how bad do you want to do this?" 8+: keep going. 7 or below: "Why not a 10?" and pivot to a different offer (feature downsell). See [models-temperature-check].
5. **Three payments.** One-third now and one-third on each of the next two paydays (or months).
6. **Even payments across the service term.** E.g. Gym Launch's 16-week program billed weekly, 16 times.
7. **Free trial** (with penalty; see [models-downsell-trial-with-penalty]) in exchange for a card on file.

**Origin.** In his first month in business, a prospect could not pay anything until the 1st; he took her card and charged it on the 2nd, three days before his rent was due. It worked, and payment plans have since made him tens of millions.

**When to use.** Any offer with a price objection, at any ticket size. If running up to nine offers in one conversation sounds crazy, you are probably serving fewer customers than you could.
