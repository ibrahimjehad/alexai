The name decides whether the ideal prospect notices and responds; renaming the same offer refreshes it when it fatigues.

A Grand Slam Offer nobody hears about makes no money. People respond far more to a "free six-week stress release challenge" than to a "float tank session", though the service is the same. People are drawn to what resembles them (the implicit egotism effect), so names should call out the avatar.

**Fatigue math.** Reaching 1,000 people costs about $20 on most platforms, so a 200,000-person local area can be reached once for about $10,000. Reaching people once does not fatigue an offer (most do not notice the first time), so keep producing new creative, hooks, stories and copy. Over years, not months, the offer itself fatigues; then rename it. Only the wrapper changes: deliverables, pricing and money model stay the same. Hormozi calls this the key to evergreen lead generation and says better names can lift response 2x, 3x or even 10x.

**When to use.** When launching an offer, and whenever lead flow drops on an offer that used to convert.
