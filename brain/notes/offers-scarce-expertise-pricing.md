Experts command very high fees because value and rarity compound: the problem is worth a lot and very few people can solve it.

Authorities and celebrities can charge egregious rates because of implied demand: people assume their time is in demand and short in supply. A buyer with a rare, consuming problem may see only one possible solver (supply = 1). If a $50,000 day yields $500,000/month more revenue within three months, it is a great deal: the client skips years of errors and cost.

Hormozi was offered $50,000 for a day twice by owners stuck at $1M/month in a business type he was running at $1M/week. He declined because his business earned more per day and he did not want the distraction. That indifference is hard to fake and is exactly why such people can charge so much.

**Principle:** the party who needs the exchange less has the upper hand, in pricing and negotiation.

**When to use.** When positioning specialist services: narrow to a rare, valuable problem and avoid signalling that you need the sale.
