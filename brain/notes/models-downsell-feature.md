Lower the price by changing what customers get (less quantity, lower quality, cheaper alternatives, or removing components), never by discounting the same thing.

**Why it works.** Every feature has a price and a value. Remove something and both fall; the customer weighs money saved against value lost. Remove what they dislike and drop the price a little, and the downsell is a better deal. Remove what they love and drop the price a little, and the original offer suddenly looks like the better deal, so many re-upsell themselves.

**Formula.** Take something away, lower the price, and in so many words ask "How about now?"

**Order.** Remove features from highest to lowest value. The first downsell is meant to make them reconsider the original offer; later ones find the best deal for them.

**Four types (with sample lines).**
- *Quantity*: "Instead of a three-month supply, start with one." "Instead of four sessions a month, start with two."
- *Quality*: "Instead of leather seats, vinyl." "Instead of five-minute response times, overnight."
- *Remove a feature*: "Keep chat and email support but drop the calls, and we pass the savings to you."
- *Done-for-you to do-it-yourself*: chiropractor to home tools (foam rollers, mats); painter to paint plus a sprayer rented by the day; acquiring and growing a company to a workshop.

**Rules.**
- Never negotiate price. People who want the same thing for less are "business terrorists"; pay less now = payment plan, pay less overall = fewer features.
- Stay a helpful guide: collaborative, not competitive, and you can make many offers without exhausting the customer.
- Standardize: as you learn what customers value, pre-set feature combinations and name them.
- After each downsell ask "Deal?" or "Fair enough?"; few people say "no, that's not fair".
- Temperature check after two refusals; alternate feature and payment-plan downsells. Using both makes you hard to refuse.

**When to use.** Price objections where the customer wants to pay less overall; current customers not using features they pay for.

**Transcript note.** The summary says "remove stuff they hate and lower the price a lot", while the body says "a little"; the principle (removed value vs price drop decides which offer looks best) is the same.
