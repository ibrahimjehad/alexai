Three compact money-model blueprints showing how the offer types chain together in different industries.

**Microgym (local business).**
1. Attraction: win your money back (pay to enter a fitness challenge, money back for hitting goals). Downsell: payment plan (split pay, three pay), then free trial with penalty.
2. Upsell: menu upsell of supplement bundles ("you won't get the best results without the right supplements"), big bundle personalized to the goal. Downsell: feature downsell (big bundle, small bundle, monthly subscription).
3. Continuity: rollover upsell plus lifetime discount, $50/month off with a 12-month commitment.

**Newsletter (digital).**
1. Attraction: free trial, $0 then a monthly price after 30 days.
2. Upsell + continuity: pay less now or pay more later plus lifetime discount ("pay now and keep that rate for life"). One offer acting as attraction, upsell and continuity at once.
Prices are spoken as "3.99" and "2.97"; given the newsletter example elsewhere in the book ($399/month), these are probably $399 and $297.

**Dog food (physical product).**
1. Attraction: buy X, get Y free (buy four months, get two free).
2. Upsell: classic upsell (monthly delivery? dog toys? vitamins?).
3. Downsell: feature downsell ("just the premium food then? You don't want anything else, do you?").
4. Continuity: automatic renewal month to month after the six-month bulk purchase; cancel anytime.

**When to use.** As starting templates when sketching a client's money model.
