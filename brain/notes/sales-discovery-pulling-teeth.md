Most sales are lost in discovery; dig with "give me an example / be more specific" until you hit a concrete problem, label it as something you sell, get agreement, and only then pitch.

**Goal.** Match their problems to your products. Closed or cold prospects give empty answers ("business isn't growing") that don't give you enough to match.

**Steps.**
1. Ask open problem questions: "What have you done so far? How long have you struggled with this?"
2. **Pull teeth.** Alternate "Can you give me an example?" and "Can you be more specific?" until something concrete appears ("I have the same number of leads as last year").
3. **Chunk up.** Recap and infer, using categories you sell: "So you're not getting leads consistently. Sounds like you have a marketing issue. Fair?"
4. "What else is holding back growth?" Repeat until you have two or three chunked-up problems.
5. **Transition.** "Before I dive in: you said inconsistent leads (marketing) and thin profit per sale (pricing and packaging). If we solved those, would it help grow the business?" A yes means they've accepted the sale before the pitch. "Can I tell you how we'd help, given what you told me?"
6. **Match each problem to a solution** with a credibility line, ending in a yes question: "Do you think sitting down with our marketing director who did X would help with your lead flow?"
7. "Then I think you'd be a great fit. Ready to rock and roll?" (Add a three-pillar pitch if you use one.) Make the invitation and stop talking.

**When to use.** The Label/Overview part of CLOSER, especially with low-energy or one-word prospects.
