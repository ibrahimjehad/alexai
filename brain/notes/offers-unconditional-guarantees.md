An unconditional (no-questions-asked) guarantee is the strongest and simplest form; it works best when you deliver well and the ticket is low.

**What the client can get:** full refund; 50% refund; refund of ad spend or other ancillary costs; you pay for a competitor's program; refund plus extra money (e.g. an additional $1,000). Adding conditions quickly blunts it.

**Framing (paraphrasing Jason Fladlien's webinar pitch):** do not ask them to decide yes or no today; ask them to make a fully informed decision, which is only possible from the inside (like seeing a house before buying it). If at any point, minutes or weeks later, they are unhappy, they email support and get their money back, with fast stated response times. Only make such a promise when you are confident in the product.

**Satisfaction-based refund (Hormozi's weight-loss guarantee).** He did not guarantee the weight-loss result ("I can't eat the food for you") but guaranteed a stated level of service and value (e.g. $500 worth); if the client felt they did not get it, he would write a check the day they said so. Only two of about 4,000 buyers over 3.5 years claimed it.

**Risk.** You are accountable even when failure is the client's fault; riskier as ticket price and fulfillment cost rise. Use only if you reliably deliver.

**When to use.** Lower-ticket B2C offers with strong fulfillment.
