Deliverables built once and reused (tools, templates, guides, software) usually have the largest gap between cost and value.

Hormozi's spreadsheet tool took about 100 hours to build: it generated 100 meals matched to a client's calorie and macro needs plus exact grocery lists and bulk-prep instructions. Afterwards each "personalized" plan took 15 minutes and sold at a high price. His gym meal plans, built in a week or two, have since been used by 4,000+ gyms and hundreds of thousands of people. This logic is why software is so valuable, and why a book is high value at low marginal cost.

High upfront cost, near-zero marginal cost: these assets become the bedrock of a scalable model. One-on-one still has a place, but only for the highest-value moments.

**When to use.** When an offer depends on owner time or one-on-one labor: identify the repeated parts and turn them into reusable assets.
