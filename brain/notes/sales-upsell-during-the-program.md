Sell the next offer while customers are still "in flight" - at the halfway point, again at two-thirds, with a last chance at the end - instead of only after they finish.

**Benchmarks (Hormozi's rules of thumb).** Really selling only at the end gets about a third to take it; selling at halfway and two-thirds gets about half to two-thirds or more. A coach who only mentioned the back end at the end was getting ~10%.

**Why.** Everyone cools off over time; sell while pain and excitement are high. It's a lighter sale when they're mid-decision ("let's keep the party going") than when they must make a new decision after finishing. Analogy: a runner buying shoes before a marathon will add a tank top and armband; after the race, they're done. Owners fear customers will say "but I'm not done yet"; they've already got value, so it doesn't work that way.

**Patterns.**
- Swim school: halfway through 12 sessions, "she's finished phase one of six", credit the front-end price toward the back end, downsell to phase two if needed.
- Structural alternative: make the offer renew automatically (e.g., a six-month term that rebills), so they opt out rather than opt in; still give a reminder push so no one is billed without knowing ("the legal right here, the psychological right from here").

**When to use.** Any program, cohort or package with a follow-on or renewal.
