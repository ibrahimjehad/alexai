When a prospect says "it's too expensive", you did a poor job showing value, and value in their mind is mostly how well you described their problem back to them.

**The idea.** If someone could tell you, psychically, exactly what you dreamed, what you fear and what's really stopping your business, you'd believe them when they said "I can solve that" without hearing a word about the product. Accuracy of diagnosis creates belief and value.

**The Bentley test.** If you were certain someone would hand you the keys to a Bentley for $5,000, you would find the $5,000 even if you didn't have it. When people believe value vastly exceeds price, they find a way: a loan, a partner, a card. "I don't have the money" usually means "it isn't worth that to me" (Ep 33: not enough pain engaged; you offered a solution to a problem you didn't establish).

**Implications.**
- Fix price objections upstream, in clarify, label and the pain cycle ([sales-pain-cycle]), not with a better rebuttal.
- Restate their situation so precisely they feel understood ([sales-three-things-to-say]).
- Bragging about your company ("we're a $100M business") adds nothing; prospects only care about themselves.

**When to use.** Diagnosing why price objections are frequent; training reps (see [sales-train-on-the-prospect-not-the-product]).
