Premium prices create better clients, better results and more money to reinvest in delivery, which justifies the premium; low prices run the same loop in reverse.

**Raising price:**
1. Increases client emotional investment.
2. Increases perceived value.
3. Improves client results, because invested clients follow through.
4. Attracts the best clients: easiest to satisfy, cheaper to serve, most likely to perceive value.
5. Multiplies margin to fund systems, talent, customer experience and scale (and owner profit).

**Lowering price** does the opposite: less investment, lower perceived value, worse results, the worst clients (never satisfied until it is free), and no margin to deliver anything exceptional.

**Supporting effects.**
- Price changes perceived value directly: in a wine study, tasters rated identical wine better when shown a higher price.
- Pricing far above market (not slightly above) signals "something different is going on here", creating a category of one with monopoly-like margins.
- For services that need customer effort, a price that "stings" focuses attention: those who pay the most pay the most attention, so they get better results, which is more real value.

Caveat: the product must deliver. Conviction to charge big prices comes from having delivered many times. Hormozi claims 99% of businesses need to raise prices, not lower them; "profit is oxygen" for growth.

**When to use.** When an owner fears raising prices or believes low prices are kinder to customers.
