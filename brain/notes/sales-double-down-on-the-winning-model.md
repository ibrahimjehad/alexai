If one model is already growing, every side opportunity is what stops it from getting as big as it could; pick one and trade novelty for loyalty.

**Stories.**
- A roofer also doing general contracting and house flipping "so as not to leave money on the table": what stops a billion-dollar roofing company? The contracting and flipping, and vice versa.
- Hormozi's own mistake: launching Prestige Labs supplements through Gym Launch's gym base slowed the main licensing business; that energy should have gone into the core.
- A trucking company growing $5M -> $7M -> $10M asked about also selling the chemicals it hauled (adding cash constraint and complexity). Ask: can I build a $100M trucking business? If yes, everything that isn't that is the risk.
- A founder with a publishing business, adventures, a TV show and a business school: do the one customers keep coming to you for (books).

**Principles.**
- The "woman in the red dress" (new opportunity) gets more attractive the more distribution and skill you build.
- You have to say no to nearly every opportunity to make one worth pursuing. Entrepreneurs only get a few big swings in a career.
- Channel creative energy into parts of the business that need novelty: marketing, content, ads ("paint the city, don't destroy it").

**When to use.** Whenever an owner with a working model proposes a new product line, market or business. Wanting to do something different is a life decision, not a business one.
