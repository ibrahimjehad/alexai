Cover the cost of getting (and serving) a customer within 30 days; a $100M money model goes further and funds at least two more customers from each one in that window.

**What this book adds** to [leads-client-financed-acquisition] (which sets the 30-day CAC + fulfillment payback rule and the credit-card logic):
- **Two tiers.** Good money model: 30-day profit per customer > cost to acquire and serve that customer. $100M money model: 30-day profit from one customer > cost to acquire and serve *many* (at least two) customers, so cash stops limiting growth.
- **Credit-card cycle restated.** Any business can borrow interest-free for about 30 days on a card. Acquire a customer on credit, collect enough within 30 days to clear the balance, repeat.
- **Speed matters as much as size.** The faster customers pay, the faster you can reinvest in the next customer while staying profitable.
- **How to get there.** Attraction offers bring cash in; upsells and downsells raise 30-day cash; continuity adds recurring cash on top (see [models-three-stages-of-money-model]).

**Check.** 30-day gross profit per new customer / (CAC + cost to deliver) >= 1 is the minimum; >= 2 is the target.

**When to use.** Setting monetization goals and deciding which offer to add next: always ask what 30-day profit per customer is today.
