You can't make something easy, only remove what makes it hard; list everything that sucks about existing providers, eliminate each, and sell the difference instead of the price.

**Steps.**
1. Stop believing your service is a commodity; you have to believe it first.
2. List every reason bad providers are bad: impersonal, poor service, incomplete jobs, won't come back, risks the customer bears if it's done badly.
3. Fix each one.
4. Say it simply: "Here are the five things that suck about this service. We have none of them. So instead of paying this, you pay this, and here's why it's worth it."
5. Put it into every outbound channel (door-to-door, email, texts).

**Example (restaurant exhaust-hood cleaning, ~6 months in, ~$100k annual book).** Owners only cared about price and the founder was undercutting. Hormozi: answer "why are we different?" before thinking about price. Consider structuring service to need fewer visits at a higher price per visit (higher gross margin, lower annual cost to the client); sell more frequency first and downsell (start at four cleanings a year, downsell to two); collect more cash up front; later raise route density to lift margin.

**When to use.** Any "everyone does the same thing" service, especially B2B owners who buy on price. Builds on [offers-commodity-vs-category-of-one].
