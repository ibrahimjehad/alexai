A fill-in-the-blank warm offer that asks whether they know someone who needs help, rather than asking them to buy, so it never feels pushy.

## Value equation reminder (from *$100M Offers*)
Maximise **dream outcome** and **perceived likelihood of achievement**; minimise **time delay** and **effort and sacrifice**. Get as close to the ideal as you can without lying or exaggerating.

## Script structure (paraphrased)
"By the way, do you know anyone who is [struggle] and wants [dream outcome] in [time]? I'm taking on five case studies for free, because that's all I can handle, so I can get testimonials. I help people get [dream outcome] without [effort/sacrifice]. I even guarantee it, or I keep working with them until they get it. I just worked with [name], who had [the same struggle as the contact] and got [result], and a guy who got [result] on his first try. I'd like more testimonials across different situations. Does anyone come to mind?"
- Pause. If they say no: "Ha, well, does anyone you *hate* come to mind?" (breaks the awkwardness).

## Why it works
- You aren't asking them to buy. Of those who say yes, most turn out to be interested themselves. Outcomes: they're interested, they refer someone, or both. You win in all three without pushing.
- Perceived likelihood is implied: pick the testimonial closest to the contact's situation and let them connect the dots. The longer you're in business, the more perfect-fit testimonials you have.

## Short version (when space or time is tight)
"I help [ideal customer] get [dream outcome] in [time period] without [effort and sacrifice]" plus one likelihood booster. Works for email, text, DM, calls and elevator pitches.

## When to use
Step 6 of warm outreach; also a model for content and cold-outreach offers.
