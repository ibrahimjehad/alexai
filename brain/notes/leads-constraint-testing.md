Every step before purchase is a drop-off point; put your testing where the most leads drop off, because the smallest improvement there creates the biggest gain.

## The idea
Constraints are the steps where the smallest improvement gives the biggest boost. Equal absolute improvements yield very different relative gains depending on where they're applied.

## Worked example
Funnel: 30% opt in → 5% apply → 50% schedule. Add 5 points to each:
- Opt-in 30% → 35% = ~16% more leads.
- Apply 5% → 10% = 100% more (2x) leads.
- Schedule 50% → 55% = 10% more.
The constraint (apply rate) wins by far.

## Front-to-back rule
The lowest percentages are usually at the front (e.g. ~1% click an ad, then ~30% opt in), so you'll mostly test front-end elements, in the order the lead experiences them: call-out, value elements, offer, CTA, landing page headline, subheadline, image, etc.

## Steps
1. Map each step and its conversion rate.
2. Find the step with the most drop-off.
3. Run tests there until improvements stall, then move to the next constraint.

## When to use
The "better" step of More-Better-New; any funnel optimization.
