For do-it-yourself or done-with-you offers, pitch the solution as three simple pillars, one analogy each, then recap and ask for the sale.

**The idea.** The goal of the pitch is to talk as little as possible and get understanding as fast as possible, so the conversation returns to the prospect. Nearly anything can be chunked into three: fitness, nutrition, accountability; leads that are timely, exclusive and qualified; grass that is cut, healthy and resilient; environment, expertise, process (swim school, Ep 953). Why three? Hormozi says he doesn't know; it has just worked.

**Two ways to set up the pillars.**
1. **Sequence** - step one, two, three (before, during, after); you can't do step two before step one. See [sales-three-term-contingency].
2. **Requirements** - you need all three at once or you won't get the result (fitness, nutrition and accountability are not a sequence).

**Steps.**
1. Name each pillar in a word or short phrase.
2. Under each, give the one analogy that makes them say "I get it" ([sales-pitch-analogies]); have one to three ready, but don't throw nine.
3. Move on as soon as they understand.
4. Recap: "Do you see why you need step one, two and three / all three to get [result]?" A yes means they have essentially closed themselves.
5. Ask for the sale. Everything after is asking again and handling obstacles (money, decision maker, past experience, avoidance).

**Ep 305 adds.** His reason for three there: the human brain can't take more than three. Because the prospect admitted at the start that they have an unsolved problem, they must be missing at least one pillar, so you're in an "unlosable" position unless you drop the frame; tie each pillar to what their past attempts lacked ([sales-missing-piece-not-your-fault]). Transition line: "This is what you were missing. Would you want to hear how the program works? I think we might be able to solve that." Keep the whole pitch under about three minutes; people buy because of how understood they feel, not the pitch.

**When to use.** The S step of CLOSER for DIY or done-with-you offers. For done-for-you services use the detailed pitch instead ([sales-pitch-style-by-delivery-model]).
