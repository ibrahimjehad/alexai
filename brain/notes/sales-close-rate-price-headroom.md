A high close rate means money left on the table: about 30-40% is appropriately priced; above that, raise price; below 30%, sell better.

**Headroom table (close rate -> likely price increase available).**
| Close rate | Price headroom |
|---|---|
| 80%+ | 3-4x |
| 60-80% | 2-3x |
| 50-60% | 1.5-2x |
| 40-50% | 1.25-1.5x |
| 30-40% | priced about right |
| under 30% | get better at selling: improve the offer or talk to better (qualified) customers |

**Maths.** At 80% close, a 4x price that drops close to 35% still yields more revenue (0.35 x 4 = 1.4 vs 0.8, about 75% more). Hormozi's on-air figure ("120%") understates it; the direction is the point.

**Finding price from scratch.** If you can't decide, start at 20% of your intended price, raise 20% every five customers, and stop when you close about one in three. Then keep raising over time while demand exceeds supply, which funds the virtuous cycle (higher margin -> better talent -> better service -> reputation -> demand -> price). Before you are confident, use a "free or full price" approach: deliver free until you're sure, then charge full price.

**Qualification matters.** Close rate is relative to who you talk to; restricting to qualified buyers (e.g., only $1M+ businesses) raises close rate at a given price.

**When to use.** Any pricing review; also when an owner brags about a very high close rate.
