Scarcity limits quantity; urgency limits time. A real deadline lowers the threshold to act now. "Deadlines drive decisions."

**Four methods.**
1. **Rolling cohorts.** Start clients on a fixed cadence (weekly, biweekly, quarterly) even if unlimited: "sign up today and you start Monday with the next group; otherwise you wait." Optional nudge: a spot opened because someone dropped out. The less often you start clients, the stronger the pull. It also enables a choreographed onboarding.
2. **Rolling seasonal urgency.** Real, dated promotions that change each month (see seasonal wrappers).
3. **Pricing or bonus urgency.** The deadline applies to the promotion, discount or bonus, not to the service: "this promotion changes every four weeks or so and it's one of the better ones." Honest for businesses that sell year-round (a roofer will still serve you later; the deal will not). Swap discounts, free install, free onboarding or a bonus workshop around the core offer.
4. **Exploding opportunity.** If the opportunity itself decays with time (arbitrage, a new ad platform before competitors arrive, exploding job offers), emphasize that every day of delay costs gains.

**Why fear of lost sales is unfounded.** In a week-long launch, 50-60% of sales typically come in the last four hours, the final 3% of the time. Those who miss a deadline were usually never going to buy.

**Someone wants in just after a cohort starts:** either add a speedy personal onboarding as a bonus, or (preferred) sell the advantage of waiting: more prep time and a longer payment plan available only because the start date is far out.

Countdowns must be real or you lose credibility.

**When to use.** In every offer: attach a genuine deadline to start date, promotion or bonus.
