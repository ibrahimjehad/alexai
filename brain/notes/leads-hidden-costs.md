If people turn down your free offer, your "free" thing is still too expensive because of hidden costs: the time, effort and sacrifice needed to get results.

## The idea
The priciest part of what you sell is often not the price but the bottom half of the value equation. If you struggle to give it away, then either they don't want the outcome (dream outcome), don't believe you (perceived likelihood), or the hidden costs (time, effort, sacrifice) are too high.

## Steps
1. When someone says no, ask why: "What would I have to do to make it worth it for you to continue?"
2. Solve the problem they name. If you do, they'll probably buy, and even if not, you have ammunition for the next prospect.
3. Each hidden cost you remove is value you can later charge for.
4. Rack up nos fast to pay your "no tax". Yeses give opportunity; nos give feedback.

**Buffett and Graham:** Warren Buffett offered to work free for Ben Graham, who said he was overpriced, because training him was the real cost. Your early customers are training you; minimise their cost. (Buffett eventually got the job.)

## When to use
When a free or cheap offer gets low take-up. Diagnose which value lever is failing before cutting price further.
