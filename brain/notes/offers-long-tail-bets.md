Business returns have an uncapped long tail, so you should take bold bets with asymmetric upside even though most will fail.

Drawing on Jeff Bezos: given a 10% chance at a 100x payoff, take the bet every time, accepting you will be wrong 9 of 10 times. Baseball caps a swing at four runs; in business one swing can score a thousand. Unlike a casino, skill in business can push the odds in your favour until you become the house.

Hormozi treats offers this way: most of the thousands he made failed, some did okay, a few struck gold. One Grand Slam Offer can be enough to retire. His benchmark: a lifetime 36:1 return on ad spend over eight years ($36 back per $1 of advertising). A failed offer means the offer was bad, not that you are; keep testing.

**When to use.** When an owner is discouraged by failed tests or hesitant to try a bolder offer: frame offers as a portfolio of experiments where one winner pays for all the misses.
