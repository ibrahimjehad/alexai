You are most convincing when the listener thinks they aren't the intended audience, so engineer moments where praise and endorsements are overheard.

**The idea.** Hearing a couple say "that guy was really nice" just after they walk round a corner hits harder than a compliment to your face. Persuasion the person believes they are privy to, rather than targeted by, is among the strongest you can build into a process.

**In a sales process.** Hormozi asked gym clients to "help me out" if they walked past while he was with a prospect. He'd ask, "Hey Leah, should she do this?" and the client would say, "Do it right now. Don't even think about it. It'll change your life," then head into class. The prospect often handed over a card on the spot.

**When to use.** In-person sales where customers are around (gyms, studios, clinics, events). Brief your customers beforehand and keep it genuine.
