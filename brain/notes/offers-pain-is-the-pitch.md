If you can describe the prospect's pain accurately, they will usually buy.

Prospects are drawn in when they hear their problem stated precisely and picture life without it. Good writing makes the reader understand; good persuasion makes the prospect feel understood. A prospect must have a painful problem before you can charge for solving it.

**How.** Lead sales conversations and copy with a specific, vivid description of the pain (and its costs) before presenting the solution; then contrast it with the life they will have once it is gone.

**When to use.** Sales scripts, ad copy, landing pages, and as a market test: if you cannot name a sharp pain, the market may be wrong.
