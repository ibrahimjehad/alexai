A downsell is any offer made after someone says no; it tweaks how they pay or what they get to find the highest-value solution within their budget, never the same thing for less.

**Two levers.**
- *How they pay*: balance how much they pay now vs over time (payment plans, trials). Total price stays the same.
- *What they get*: change quantity, quality, or offer something different (feature downsells). Price goes down because value goes down.

**Rules.**
1. **They said no to this offer, not to all offers** (and not to you). Treat the no as information about what they really want, then make another offer.
2. **Downsells are trades.** If you give something, get something.
3. **Personalize, don't pressure.** Offer more of what they like and less of what they don't, with a price to match. Asking someone who declined a large soda whether they want a small, a juice or a coffee is service, not pushiness.
4. **Offer the same stuff in new ways.** Do not build a hundred products; find a hundred ways to offer what you have.
5. **Never just drop the price.** That is discounting, not downselling. Customers talk; if someone learns another person got the same thing cheaper "just because", you lose trust (and Hormozi considers it unethical). Wanting to pay less now = payment plan; wanting to pay less overall = feature downsell.
6. **Price tests are fine** when planned: a set price to a set number of people, decided in advance, not a panicked in-the-moment cut.

**The three processes.** Payment plan downsells and trial with penalty (how they pay), feature downsells (what they get).

**When to use.** Every sales process; know in advance what you will offer after each no.
