Almost every business problem traces back to two: not enough clients, and not enough cash (profit left at month end).

They feed each other. Getting clients costs time and money that comes out of margin. Prospects compare you with cheaper, worse alternatives and the cheapest often wins, so owners cut price, fill up with clients and still barely survive on thin margins: a race to the bottom. Hormozi argues the standard models owners copy were designed by well-funded companies that can lose money for years, which is why small operators "buy themselves a job".

The book's answer: fix pricing (charge premium), build value so the premium is a bargain, then enhance the offer so you can outspend competitors on acquisition.

**When to use.** As a diagnostic opener: ask which of the two is binding, then check whether low prices and commoditization are the root cause.
