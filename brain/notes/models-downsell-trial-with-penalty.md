Let people who said no start free on condition they do what makes customers successful; if they skip the requirements, they pay a fee.

**Contrast.** Win your money back: they pay first and earn it back by meeting the terms. Trial with penalty: they pay nothing and only pay if they fail the terms. It changes what they pay today, not the total. Hormozi prefers it as a downsell, offered only after the main offer is refused.

**Economics.** Close 3 of 10 on the up-front offer, downsell 4 more into the trial, then convert 3 of those after the trial: 3 sales become 6.

**Terms.** What they get free: your bare-bones (decoy-style) version or your win-your-money-back package; give more rather than less if you can afford it. What they must do: copy the win-your-money-back criteria, things that activate and retain customers and advertise the business.

**Examples.**
- B2C "28-day kick that habit blueprint": free if they attend all consulting calls, post progress pictures weekly, journal daily in the app, attend feedback and transformation sessions (the upsell opportunities).
- B2B "5-day get your first 5 customers challenge": 100 outbound messages/day, report stats, attend daily training, post homework, attend the graduation call.
- HR software ($500 onboarding then $99/month): no $500 up front if they attend three 60-minute onboarding calls, do the homework, activate the employer profile and set up employees by the third call; otherwise they pay the fee.

**Fees.** Either many small fees (e.g. $50 per missed item on a $500 product with 10 tasks; Hormozi's preference: you profit from results, not from nickel-and-diming) or one lump fee when a single miss wrecks success.

**Five-step sales process.**
1. **Offer the trial last**, only after a clear no: "How about we just get you started for free? If you like it, you can stay."
2. **Always get a card** (and ID): "What card do you want to use?" If they balk: "That's how we've always done it." Still no: politely end. People who will not put a card down and do the work complain more and convert less.
3. **Sell staying and paying first**: "If this gets you the result, will you stay long term?" If no, do not give the trial. Frame the trial as building habits, not reaching long-term goals.
4. **Explain fees after getting the card**, with a "this is how we've always done it" attitude: "We'll do our part if you do yours. Miss something and you get a small fee that keeps you on track; follow through and it's all free." Have customers initial the fee clause separately (forces salespeople to explain it).
5. **Make check-ins required** and list each one's purpose; they are your upsell meetings.

**Tuning.** Nobody takes the trial: lower requirements or penalties. People take it but do not follow through: better explain how fees help them and make the sales meeting mandatory. Nobody stays after: sell staying harder, deliver better, and make the back end fit the front end. Let people make up missed items before you bill.

**Naming.** Just call it a free trial; "penalty" scares people. If asked why it works this way: "People get the best results this way."

**Fit.** Use trial with penalty as the downsell for recurring products or services where customers must do work to get results; use pay less now or pay more later for physical products or one-time services.

**When to use.** After a payment-plan sequence fails, or as the last step of it.
