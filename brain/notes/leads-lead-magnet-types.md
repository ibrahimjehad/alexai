Lead magnets solve a problem in one of three ways: they diagnose a problem, give a brief sample of the core offer, or give away one step of a multi-step solution.

## The three types
1. **Reveal their problem (diagnosis).** Best when the problem gets worse the longer they wait. Draw a clear line from where they are to where they should be and what it costs them.
   - Website speed test showing a site loads 30% slower than it should, and the lost revenue.
   - Posture analysis showing what pain-free posture looks like.
   - Termite inspection: if termites, sell removal; if none, sell prevention. You sell either way.
2. **Samples and trials.** Full but brief access to the core offer, limited by uses, time, or both. Best when the core offer is a recurring solution to a recurring problem.
   - Put their site on your fast server; keep paying to keep the speed.
   - One free adjustment; lasting results require more.
   - Consumables (food, cosmetics, supplements): single-serve samples, as Costco does.
   - "Be a (legal) drug dealer": give the strongest hit first, never a diluted one, so they come back and pay for the next.
3. **One step of a multi-step process.** Give one valuable step free and the rest on purchase. Best when the core offer solves a complex problem.
   - Garage door sealant needs three coats; apply the first free, explain it only partly protects, sell the other two as a bundle.
   - Free finance courses, calculators and templates so good people could DIY, which also reveals how much time and effort DIY takes; sell the done-for-you service.

## Combine all three
The clothing-store **dressing room** does all three: it's a trial (try it on), it reveals problems (this doesn't fit, I need something else), and it's step one of a multi-step outfit ("want pants with that?"). Aim for a lead magnet that reveals a problem, gives a taste of the solution, and is shown as one piece of a total package.

## When to use
Step 2 of the lead magnet process. Match the type to the core offer: diagnosis for urgent hidden problems, trials for recurring products, single steps for complex services.
