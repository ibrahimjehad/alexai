Credit some or all of a customer's previous purchase toward a bigger offer; in Hormozi's experience far more people take it.

**Decide three things.**
1. **Who** (four situations):
   - Customers who left a while ago (re-engage; 6+ months since last purchase).
   - Your own unhappy customers (as a better alternative to a refund).
   - Competitors' unhappy customers.
   - Regular current customers (e.g. right after a short first purchase).
2. **What**: more of what they bought, something better, or something new/different. To make money, roll credit into something more expensive.
3. **How**: apply all or part of the credit, either up front or spread over time.

**Pricing rule.** Make the next offer at least 4x the credit, so even full credit is at most a 25% discount and you keep profit. You need not credit everything: use whatever amount tips them; test for the sweet spot. Bigger credit, more takers, less margin.

**Spreading example.** A $600 first purchase applied to a 12-month membership = $50/month off for 12 months. A customer who wins $600 credit on a $200/month service pays $150/month for a year rather than getting three free months up front (people fall off if they pay nothing; keeping skin in the game is in their interest). They may use credit however they want, but present the spread first.

**Examples.**
- Chiropractor win-back: patients 6+ months inactive; offer $500 of past spend as credit toward a new plan ("I wanted to give you your money back...").
- Dentist save: patient unhappy that one $200 cleaning did not whiten; credit the $200 to a whitening package (multiple sessions, home kit, deep cleanings).
- Software: find competitors' unhappy customers (e.g. from negative reviews) and credit what they still owe on their old contract toward a longer agreement with you.
- Membership: someone buys a small block of time; immediately offer to apply the whole amount toward 12 months.

**Rules.** Roll over before refunding ("roll over for a do-over"); add urgency by making it a one-time offer, available only in that moment; if they decline they can still pay full price later.

**When to use.** Right after any front-end purchase, for win-back campaigns, refund requests, competitor conquest, and cancellation saves.
