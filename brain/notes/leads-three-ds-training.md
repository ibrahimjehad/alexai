Turn a task you already do into a checklist, show it, then have the trainee do it in front of you, fixing the checklist until a stranger could get your results from it alone.

## Steps
1. **Document** — write the steps exactly as you do them (or have an observer document you; better yet, record yourself doing it several ways across shifts and watch as an observer). Then do your next work block following only the checklist. If you get an A+ result, that's draft one.
2. **Demonstrate** — you do it in front of them, walking through the checklist step by step. Wherever they stop you or slow down, adjust the checklist. Draft two.
3. **Duplicate** — they do it in front of you following the checklist. If the checklist is right, the outcome matches; if not, you'll find out fast. Fix it until it's right and they nail it.
Target clarity: if you vanished tomorrow, could a stranger get your results using only the checklist?

## Training rules
- If they get it wrong or confused, *we* got it wrong. Needing to explain a step means it's too complicated or bundles several steps.
- Competence ≠ performance: knowing what to do vs being good at it. If directions are right, they just need reps ("slow, smooth, fast").
- Judge their ability to follow directions over whether they got the result — then wrong results point to the directions, which you control.
- Praise each correct step; if they follow directions and still get the wrong result, praise them anyway and fix the checklist on the spot.
- Don't take over when they slip; pause and let them retry. Fast feedback = faster learning.
- No punishment during training; reward what you want more of.
- One piece of feedback at a time, one step at a time.
- When performance dips from normal, retrain — usually someone dropped a step they didn't know mattered; then reward following it.
- Forcing an inferior checklist to work becomes a nightmare once someone else does the training.

## Decision rule
Give short windows to prove themselves: entry-level advertising jobs take more grit than skill. If someone is properly trained and still below expectations after ~3 weeks, cut them.

## When to use
Onboarding any employee into a repeatable lead-getting (or other) task.
