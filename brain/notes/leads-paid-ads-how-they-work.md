Paid ads rent another business's audience so you reach cold strangers one-to-many; reach is guaranteed, getting your money back is not, so the game is efficiency, not reach.

## The idea
- Paid ads = paying a platform or another business to put your offer in front of *their* audience ("renting eyeballs or earballs"). No audience-building needed, so it is the fastest way to get the most people to see your stuff. You trade money for reach.
- Like cold outreach, the audience is cold and low-trust, so a smaller percentage responds even to good offers; you compensate with volume.
- With enough spend, anyone would buy, even by accident. So the question is never "do ads work?" but "how well can I make them work?" — a push and pull between what you spend and what they buy.
- When an ad is unprofitable, most of the time the right people never saw it.

## Needle in the haystack (the whole method)
Start with the whole world (haystack) and narrow toward a high percentage of engaged leads (needle):
1. Pick a platform that contains your ideal audience.
2. Use the platform's targeting to find them.
3. Craft the ad so it repels everyone else.
4. Tell whoever is left exactly what to do next.

## Scaling logic
Win profitably in a small "puddle" audience, then expand to pond, lake, ocean. Bigger audiences contain more wrong people (efficiency ratio falls) but more right people too (total profit rises). Example: $1k spend → $10k back ($9k profit) vs $100k spend → $300k back ($200k profit). Goal: make the audience as big as possible while still profitable.

## The four problems paid ads add
1. Where to advertise (platform). 2. Getting the right audience to see it (targeting). 3. Making the best ad for them (call out + value + CTA). 4. Getting permission to contact them (landing page).

## When to use
As the frame for any paid-ads plan. Hormozi recommends doing paid ads last of the Core Four: the skills from the other three transfer, and they generate the money paid ads require.
