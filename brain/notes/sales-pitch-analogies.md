Tie each pitch point to something the prospect already understands, using the largest experience your audience shares.

**How.** Relate the unknown (your product) to the known (a story, metaphor or analogy), so they have a frame to hang the idea on. Pick analogies from your audience's world (don't use football for an audience that doesn't follow football). Use the one that lands, then move on.

**Examples.**
- SEO vs ads: ads are a paycheck (stop working, stop getting paid); SEO is an investment account (small at first, eventually out-earns active work). Three of four elements are already familiar.
- Accountability: as a kid your parents made you brush your teeth; now you do it yourself. External accountability became internal habit, and that is what we do for this new habit.
- Expertise: who teaches drawing better and faster, a high-school kid who draws or a master artist?

**Ep 305 / Ep 275 add.** The teeth-brushing story above first appears in Ep 305. More from those episodes:
- Nutrition: "Do you need motivation to watch your favourite TV show? No, you look forward to it. We'll get you to look forward to your food."
- Exercise: "If you liked working out more than watching TV, which would you do? Our goal isn't to make you work out, it's to make you like it more than TV."
- Diet: "Could you never eat cake or cookies again for the rest of your life? Then we find foods you like more, because willpower doesn't last."
- Business owner: give a man a fish vs teach him to fish (learn marketing yourself or never be free).
Each ends with "Does that sound fair?" to lock agreement. Prepare several per belief and memorise them cold ([sales-memorize-stories-and-overcomes]).

**When to use.** Under each pillar of a three-pillar pitch; in webinars and content. Listen to your best reps: most already have these stories.
