Pitch the destination (the outcome they will live in), not the steps, features and logistics of getting there.

**The idea.** New salespeople and founders talk about the flight: check-in, TSA, bags, seats. Buyers want Maui: the beach, the wind in their hair, a drink in hand. In a restaurant analogy, talk about the full stomach with family, not how they order, the menu selection or refills.

**How.** Describe life after the result, then structure the "how" as a short three-point pitch ([sales-three-pillar-pitch]) rather than a feature list.

**Ep 20 / Ep 305 add.** A copywriter friend told Hormozi his whole pitch and copy was just selling the vacation; he says to write it on the wall for the sales team. The plane flight in their world is "your modules, your meal plan, your macros, your URLs, your funnels, your targeting". Ep 305: the more tactics of the program you explain, the more details the prospect can pull at or dislike. The Maui image extends to pricing tiers: every level of service goes to the same destination, just by a different vehicle ([sales-same-destination-different-vehicle]).

**When to use.** The S step of CLOSER, ads and webinars. Exception: done-for-you services, where showing every step (to prove they don't want to do it themselves) is the pitch ([sales-pitch-style-by-delivery-model]).
