Advertise a free or cheap stripped-down version; when leads engage, present it side by side with a far more valuable premium offer and steer them to the premium.

**Why it works.** The contrast shows how much more valuable the premium is. Everyone can be closed: those who take the premium are great; those who take the decoy are still customers you can upgrade later instead of losing them. Cheap and profitable acquisition that any business can run. Underlying skill: know what gets customers results better than they do, which makes the premium the obvious choice. Philosophy (from Hormozi's mentor John): give customers what they want now so you can give them what they need later.

**Steps.**
1. Build the decoy from your premium offer: fewer components, older models, less personalization, and no guarantee. Strip it as far as reasonable.
2. Make the premium as good as possible: more features, benefits, bonuses, guarantees.
3. Advertise the decoy's benefit and dream outcome ("21-day transformation"), not features; details come in the sales conversation.
4. When leads engage, present both, emphasizing the premium (premium first if possible; see [models-decoy-presentation-script]).

**Examples.**
- Lemonade: ad "free (or $1) week of lemonade"; decoy = powdered mix; premium = organic, cold-pressed imported lemons delivered, extra flavors.
- Float tank centre: ad "free (or $6) six-week stress release"; decoy = one float/month plus DIY exercises; premium = two floats/week for six weeks, one-on-one consulting, journal, sleep routine, satisfaction guarantee.
- Gym: ad "free (or $21) 21-day transformation"; decoy = once-a-day workouts in an online group, generic nutrition plan, recordings, no support or guarantee; premium = unlimited workouts, personalized nutrition, one-on-one accountability, results guarantee or another 21 days free.

**Results.** Hormozi's gyms: free once-a-week option vs premium (unlimited sessions, coaching, results-or-repeat guarantee); 70 to 80% took the premium.

**Tuning.** If you are not making money fast, widen the contrast between the two offers.

**When to use.** Rising lead costs, any business that can strip down its core offer; easy to train salespeople on.

**Pitfalls.** Decoy too good (no contrast); advertising features instead of outcomes; refusing to present the decoy when asked (if they ask about it, you must present it).
