Give products or service time free in exchange for committing to a longer paid term (buy X, get Y free, continuity style); decide how you apply the discount and your cancellation policy.

**Four ways to apply the discount.**
1. **Up front**: free time first, then the paid term starts. Best where contracts are enforceable (phones, storage, real estate, equipment, anything with collateral). Skip if churn is historically high. Gets customers but delays cash.
2. **At the end**: if they make every payment on time, they earn bonus time equal to the discount at the end of the term. Lower churn, fewer conversions.
3. **Spread over the term**: e.g. three months free on a one-year commitment at $200/month = $600, applied as $50/month off for 12 months. Optionally let them keep the discount for life if every payment was on time. Keeps cash flowing.
4. **After the first payment or two**: collect some cash to cover advertising and delivery, then apply the discount. Present it as "first and last month up front" or add an activation fee before the bonus; also confirms the payment method works.

**Trade-off.** Front-loaded discounts convert more but churn more; back-loaded convert fewer but churn less.

**Extend the term, don't eat into it.** "Three months free with a year" should mean 12 paid + 3 free = 15 months, not 9 paid + 3 free. Then you can feature-downsell to a shorter term.

**Gift-card version.** Give the free time as a physical gift card usable after the first few payments, or giftable to a friend: it becomes a lead magnet, and many forget to use it (a full-price sign-up).

**Fits.** Internet, pool cleaning, gyms, landscaping, anything rentable.

**When to use.** Recurring services that want commitment; when competitors sell month to month.
