To make money you need something to sell (an offer), people to sell it to (leads), and a way to get them to buy (sales); skipping straight to "make money" fails.

## The idea
- *$100M Offers* covers the first piece (an offer so good people feel stupid saying no). *$100M Leads* assumes that offer exists and answers the next problem: who do I sell it to?
- Strangers can only buy if they know you exist. **Advertising** is simply "the process of making known": letting strangers know about the stuff you sell. More people knowing means more sales.
- A lead, in plain terms, is someone with the problem to solve and the money to spend. Leads do not appear on their own: you go get them, or more precisely you help them find you, and you can force that through advertising instead of waiting.
- Why the skill matters so much: lots of leads lets you survive a weak product, weak selling and many mistakes. Lead generation buys you repeated second chances, which are rare in business. Hormozi's maxim: it's hard to be poor with leads banging down your door.
- Scope boundary: the leads book ends at a "raised hand" (someone showing interest). Converting that interest to a purchase is sales, a separate skill.

## When to use
Use as the diagnostic frame when a business is stuck: check offer first, then lead flow, then sales conversion, and work on the weakest piece. If the offer is weak, fix it before scaling leads.
