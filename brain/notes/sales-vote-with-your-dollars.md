"I don't have the money" usually means it isn't a priority yet, so agree, tell a story through someone just like them, and show that new identities come with new spending priorities.

**Script.**
1. **Agree.** "I totally understand."
2. **Talk past them.** Never talk about their circumstances directly; talk about someone just like them who came in yesterday, reached the same conclusion, and heard a story that helped. "Mind if I share it?"
3. **The principle.** "We vote with our dollars for the things we care about. What we say we care about doesn't matter; where we spend is what we really care about. To change what we care about, we change how we vote with our dollars."
4. **The story.** Hormozi's example: a 14-year-old at a makeup counter is told she'll need to budget for this every month now, and she's thrilled. The purchase marked her step from girl to woman, and the new identity came with new priorities.
5. **Bridge.** "This isn't girl to woman; it's going from unhappy with how you look and feel to someone who is. That fit person spends money on health and coaching." Note the savings the new identity brings (less drinking out, junk food, doctor visits).
6. **Break it down.** "It's really about $10 a day." Say it plainly: "It's not a budget issue, it's a priorities issue."
7. Close.

**Why it suits recurring offers.** Front-end offers sell emotionally; ongoing payments are harder because a permanent fix brings a permanent cost, and people would rather pay $2,000 for 12 weeks than $150/month for a year. Introduce the ongoing cost as part of the identity change (see [models-continuity-vs-standalone-pricing] for pricing).

**When to use.** Money objections in consumer sales, especially memberships and subscriptions.
