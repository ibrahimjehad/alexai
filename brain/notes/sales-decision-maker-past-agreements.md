When the prospect says they need to talk to a spouse or partner, lean on the agreement that partner has already made: they know there's a problem and they don't like it.

**Steps (Hormozi's sequence).**
1. **"What if they say no?"** With some (often male) business owners this is an ego pull: "What if they don't give you permission?" "I don't need permission." "Then let's rock and roll."
2. **Past agreements.** If they say "then I wouldn't do it": "Totally understand. Does your partner agree there's a problem? Are they happy with how things are right now?" "No." "Then why would they be against fixing something they already don't approve of?"
3. **Forgiveness over permission.** Sometimes add, lightly, that it's better to ask forgiveness than permission (depends on ticket size).
4. **Delayed close (last resort).** "Let's get your card down as a deposit and pay the rest on Friday. If your partner says 'no, I want to keep struggling', I'll tear up the contract."

**Theory.** A decision-maker objection usually means something went wrong earlier in the sale (best fixed by qualifying for authority up front, see [sales-seven-components-of-a-closed-deal]); once you're in the "red zone", rely on past agreements rather than arguing.

**When to use.** Any sale where the buyer defers to a spouse, co-founder or boss. For consumer offers, a short cancellation window works similarly ([sales-spouse-cooling-off-guarantee]).
