Every sale reduces to two yes/no questions: if this does what I say, is it worth the price? And what would you need to see to believe it will work for you? Two yeses close the sale.

**Question 1: value (the promise).** "If this does what I say it does and gets you to your goal, is it worth this price?" If no, build value ([sales-stack-value-in-the-gap]).

**Question 2: belief (the delivery).** "Then what would you need to see to believe it can happen for you?" Asking it directly stops the vague "I need to think about it", because that isn't an answer to what you asked. Then do the conviction work: testimonials and social proof first ([sales-pre-appointment-testimonial-page]), then any limiting belief ([sales-limiting-belief-customer-stories]).

**Diagnosis.** A no means one of two things: they don't see the promise as valuable enough, or they don't believe you can deliver it.

**B2B vs B2C.**
- **B2B:** value is easy ("pay us $10k, we make you $100k; worth it?" "Sure"); belief is the hard part.
- **B2C / emotional sales (weight loss):** value is the harder part, so you dig into the pain and stack value; belief is "medium hard".

**Why competitors lose.** In most markets everyone makes roughly the same promise (more leads, more customers, more money), so claims escalate. The seller who wins is the one who addresses question 2 with proof, not a bigger promise.

**Conflict note.** Ep 20 says prospects almost always say yes to question 1; the next day (Ep 21) Hormozi corrects himself: that's true for B2B, but in emotional sales you often have to build the value first.

**When to use.** As the simplest version of CLOSER for teams ([sales-closer-framework]): Clarify through Sell the vacation answer question 1; proof and objection handling answer question 2.
