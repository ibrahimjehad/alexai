To get people to buy two products together, offer A, B and A+B, with A+B priced the same as the more expensive single option.

**Case.** The Economist offered digital for $59/year and digital plus print for $125; print sales collapsed as readers chose digital only. Adding "print only, $125" next to "digital plus print, $125" made the bundle the obvious choice and customers took it.

**Rule.** Price the options to protect margin; the bundle at the single-item price makes the choice easy.

**When to use.** When you want customers to take two products (online and offline, product and service) rather than the cheaper one.
