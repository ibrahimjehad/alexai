Cold outreach is slow, tedious and volume-heavy, but it has seven big advantages that make it worth building.

## The seven
1. **No need for lots of content or ads.** Perfect one message and improve it daily; no ad fatigue or banner blindness, since prospects haven't seen you. No marketing genius needed.
2. **Competitors can't see it.** It's private, so you don't teach rivals how you get customers (Hormozi only learned of a $10M/month competitor because they cold-contacted him).
3. **Very reliable.** Input predicts output. Build the equation "every X contacts gives Y customers" and solve for X. E.g. 1 customer per 100 emails means 100 customers need 10,000 emails, ~333 a day; at ~111 emails per person per day, that's three people.
4. **Few platform changes.** Rules for calls, doors and email have barely changed in 30 years, unlike public platform algorithms.
5. **Easier compliance.** No ad-platform claim rules or banned categories (tobacco, firearms, cannabis, weight loss); you still must follow FTC law. Hard to shut down: phone or email is enough.
6. **No spokesperson needed, so the business is sellable.** Buyers pay more when customer flow doesn't depend on the founder; the outreach team is how Gym Launch could be sold.
7. **Hard to copy.** Many steps are invisible (list scraping, personalisation, software, hiring and training), and the head start compounds.

People usually underestimate the volume and time it takes.

## When to use
When pitching a client on building an outbound team, especially if they want a sellable business, operate in a restricted ad category, or depend on a founder's personal brand.
