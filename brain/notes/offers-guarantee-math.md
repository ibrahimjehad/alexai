Judge a guarantee by net sales (sales minus refunds), not refund rate.

Example: without the guarantee, 100 sales, 5% refunds = 95 net. With a strong guarantee, 130 sales, 10% refunds (13) = 117 net, 1.23x (23% more), almost all of it profit.

**Break-even rule.** A guarantee only fails if the extra refunds wipe out the extra sales one for one, e.g. a 5-point lift in sales offset by a 5-point rise in refunds, which would mean doubling refunds. That rarely happens, so stronger guarantees usually raise net purchases even when refunds rise. "Don't be emotional, just do the math."

**When to use.** When an owner fears a strong guarantee will be abused: run the numbers with their close and refund rates.
