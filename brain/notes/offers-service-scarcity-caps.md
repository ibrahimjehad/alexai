Services can create honest scarcity by publicly capping how many clients they take.

1. **Total business cap**: "We only service 25 clients, period" (or per service tier). Build a waitlist; when a slot opens, price resistance disappears. Periodically raise capacity 10-20% then cap again, or raise prices and replace weaker accounts. Best for top tiers.
2. **Growth-rate cap**: "We take five new clients a week; three spots are already gone, and I have six more calls this week." Hormozi used this from the start because weekly onboarding capacity is real anyway.
3. **Cohort cap**: "We take 100 clients four times a year." Also adds operational cadence and gives sales legitimate scarcity.

**Honest scarcity.** You cannot take 1,000 clients tomorrow, so define how many you can take per period and advertise it: "we're three-quarters full this week" or "81% to capacity". It also implies social proof. You decide where "full" is.

**Higher-ticket items.** Workshops, events, seminars and consulting fit naturally. Release fewer spots than you think you can sell, so people remember you sold out fast.

**Lead magnets too.** A free checklist limited to 20 downloads per week, with a waitlist when it runs out, becomes more desired and more likely to be used.

**When to use.** Whenever a service has real capacity limits (almost always): state them in sales calls, pages and ads.
