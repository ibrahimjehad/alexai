What is easy to sell is usually hard to fulfill, and vice versa; start by over-delivering to get cash flowing, then make fulfillment efficient.

Doing more for the customer makes the offer easier to sell but harder to deliver; doing less makes it easier to deliver but harder to sell. The goal is the sweet spot: sells very well and is easy to fulfill.

**Mantra:** create flow, monetize flow, then add friction. Generate demand first, get people saying yes, and only then add friction to marketing or offer less for the same price. Without demand you cannot learn what to adjust.

**Gym Launch example.** Early on Hormozi flew to each gym for 21 days, paid his own travel and ads, generated and worked the leads, sold for them and ran the first onboarding. The owner put $500 down (refundable after launch) with zero financial, time or effort risk; he kept the upfront cash collected and they got the clients. It brought in about $100K/month for him and later a team of eight, but wore everyone out. Switching to teaching owners to do it themselves earned perhaps a third per gym but let him serve hundreds of gyms a month instead of eight. The promise ("I'll fill your gym in 30 days") stayed the same; only the how and what changed.

**Rule.** Over-deliver like crazy on your first Grand Slam Offer, then use the cash to build systems that create the same value with fewer resources.

**When to use.** When launching an offer (do more) and when scaling a proven one (systematize).
