Value drivers only count once the prospect perceives them; often you can raise perceived value more cheaply than real performance.

London Underground: rider satisfaction rose more from a countdown display showing when the next train arrives (a few million) than from making trains faster (billions). It cut the perceived wait and boredom, not the actual wait.

**Implication.** Communicate every improvement in likelihood, speed and ease explicitly; and look for cheap ways to change how waiting and effort feel.

**When to use.** When delivery is genuinely good but customers do not feel it, or when a real improvement is too expensive.
