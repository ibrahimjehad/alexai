A win is any positive customer experience; delivering more of them, sooner, raises perceived speed, retention and trust — which makes referring a friend feel low-risk.

## The idea
Same progress, more wins: one delivery at the end of a week vs daily progress updates = seven times the wins. Seven promises made and kept builds more trust than one.

## Five ways
1. Deliver multiple small items at shorter intervals instead of all at once.
2. Updates are wins: on big projects, update as often as possible, even with no progress — never leave customers hanging.
3. Customers form a lasting impression within ~48 hours of buying: force as many wins as possible in that window (set many expectations, meet them, repeat).
4. They should always know when they'll next hear from you — "book a meeting from a meeting."
5. Never expect forgiveness: deliver early, never late. Pad timelines by 50% so on-time for you is early for them.

## Action step
Break outcomes into the smallest increments, communicate as often as reasonable, set timelines with breathing room, deliver early.

## When to use
Onboarding and fulfillment design for any service or program.
