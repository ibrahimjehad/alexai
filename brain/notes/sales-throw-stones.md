Acknowledge that the cheap shortcut works for a while, then use their own history to show it always ends with them back where they started.

**Script (weight-loss example).**
1. Concede: "Yes, you could starve yourself for three weeks and lose the same weight."
2. "Have you dieted before?" "Yes." "So you've lost weight before?" "Yes."
3. "Then why are you here?" "Because I gained it back."
4. Project it forward: "When you're 80 looking back, do you want to say you were in shape three or four times in your life for a couple of months? If you keep doing one-off things, that's your story. Do you want to change that story?" "Yes." "Awesome, because we can do that."
5. Move into the future-story value stack ([sales-stack-value-in-the-gap]).

**Why.** They supply the evidence, so it lands as their conclusion, not your attack.

**When to use.** Whenever a prospect compares you to a cheaper, faster or DIY option they've used before. Related to ruling out alternatives in [sales-close-the-other-doors].
