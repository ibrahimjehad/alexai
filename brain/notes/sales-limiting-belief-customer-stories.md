When a prospect believes your product works and works for people like them but still hesitates, it's a limiting belief about themselves: ask what would stop them, then answer each reason with a real customer who said the same thing.

**Steps.**
1. **Surface it.** "Susan, what's the reason you believe would stop you from achieving this?" (Ep 112: "Why don't you think you'd achieve it? What feels missing?")
2. **Answer with a matching customer, then repeat** "Is that the only reason?" until none remain:
   - "I'm super busy." -> "Here are 20 working and single moms who said that sitting where you are. Sandra hired a nanny two days a week. Deborah committed to 5 a.m. and stopped telling herself she wasn't a morning person."
   - "I've never stuck with anything." -> "Forty people told me that. Motivation comes from results plus ease, and we made it so easy that 12-year-olds and people who don't speak English do it. You can read? You're older than 12? Then you'll be fine." (Humour helps.)
   - "My husband doesn't support me." -> "Monica's husband forbade her to sign up. She did anyway to prove him wrong, and he later apologised." Then: the change has to come from you.
3. **Recap your pillars** and the future story ("Don't you want the story where today was the day everything changed?"), then ask.
4. **If they see themselves as a special case**, it's usually a past failure they don't understand: explain why it failed and why this is different ([sales-missing-piece-not-your-fault]).

**Why.** People rarely believe in themselves, and the deepest limiting beliefs are ones they don't know they have until challenged. Stories let them see someone like them succeed ([sales-third-party-foil]).

**When to use.** After value is agreed and proof is shown, when hesitation remains. Hormozi calls these easy "hand-to-hand" overcomes, unlike "I don't believe you" or "too expensive", which you fix by asking the two questions differently.
