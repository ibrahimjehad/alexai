The salespeople who close the most are the ones who ask for the sale the most times, without losing rapport; objection overcomes mostly exist to give you a reasonable excuse to ask again.

**The balance.** Asking too much annoys people, so the skill is asking as many times as possible while keeping rapport. AAA ([sales-aaa-framework]) is the tool for that.

**Overcomes as excuses to ask.** If someone raises an issue, "If we solved it this way, would that work?" "Yes." "Want to get started?" For non-logistical objections (decision maker, time, money), you can memorise a handful of universal closes (he mentions seven) rather than bespoke rebuttals ([sales-reason-is-the-reason-close]). You never win a sale by being right; reframe and ask again.

**Where sales are won.** From his data, only about 20-30% of sales come after the first no. Most selling happens before the ask: everything before the call (content, offer, reputation) matters more than the call, and within the call most deals are lost in discovery ([sales-discovery-pulling-teeth]).

**When to use.** Setting expectations for reps and designing the close: plan several asks, each after a pause ([sales-pause-after-the-ask]).
