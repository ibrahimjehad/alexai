Tell customers which options they do not need (unsell), prescribe exactly what they do need and how to use it, ask which variant they prefer (A/B), and make paying easy (card on file).

**The four steps.**
1. **Unsell.** Cross out what they do not need. It builds trust and goodwill and focuses them on what they do need. Hormozi later kept products just to cross them out.
2. **Prescribe.** Tell them what they need, as if they already own it: (a) how it fits with what they already bought and (b) detailed, personalized instructions to get the most value. Removes "whether to buy" from the conversation. Works best when only one item solves the problem.
3. **A/B.** Ask which of two options they prefer (flavour, start date, quantity); either answer is a sale. Use when several items solve the same problem.
4. **Card on file.** "Do you want to just use the card on file?" Removes the friction of choosing and pulling out a card.

**Examples.**
- Massage therapist: unsell lymphatic massage (not pregnant or post-surgery); prescribe heat, trigger points, then dynamic stretches for the shoulder; A/B before work or on the way home; card on file.
- Dog food: unsell puppy food, small bags and vitamins already in the food; prescribe joint chews each meal and a heartworm wafer every 90 days, and book next month's visit; A/B beef or chicken; card on file.
- Digital product: unsell "you don't need all eight courses; I'll send free stuff for problems X and Y"; prescribe the one course for Z, an hour a day, done a particular way; A/B DMs or phone support, start today or Monday; card on file.

**Rules.**
- Unselling lower-margin items where appropriate makes customers more receptive to higher-margin ones.
- Let employees unsell and help customers "game the system"; insider tips on getting the most value build loyalty.
- Nudge new customers toward what suits them.
- Sold out? Take payment and set a delivery date; you can sell wider selection without inventory.

**When to use.** When you have multiple products or add-ons; consults, onboarding and point-of-sale conversations.
