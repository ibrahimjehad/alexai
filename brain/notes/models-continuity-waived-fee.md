Offer month to month with a startup fee of 3 to 5x the monthly rate, or waive the fee for a 12+ month commitment; cancelling early means paying the waived fee.

**Mechanics (example).** $1,000/month, 12-month term, $5,000 fee.
- Option A: pay $5,000 plus $1,000 for the first month, then $1,000/month; cancel any time.
- Option B: commit to 12 months at $1,000/month with the fee waived; pay the $5,000 only if you break the commitment.
Have customers initial that they understand they can quit early by paying the waived fee.

**Why it works.** Fees get them to start: committing gives immediate value (avoiding the fee). Fees get them to stick: early on, quitting costs a lot; later it costs about the same as finishing. If the cost to quit exceeds the cost to stay, they stay. Risk is shared: month to month, you are protected by the fee; committed, they are protected by the waiver; if they quit, they pay as if they had chosen month to month.

**Rules.**
- Justify the fee as setup cost: short-term flexibility means they cover setup; long-term commitment means you cover it.
- Fee size: 3 to 5x monthly for commitments. For more up-front cash, use a smaller fee (1.5 to 3x monthly) so more choose month to month and pay it.
- Minimum commitment: one year; works better the longer the term and for slow-results services (SEO, investing, weight loss).
- Drop the fee once the commitment is fulfilled; after the term they can cancel free.
- More than 5% wanting to cancel early means look into it: pricing should nudge, not handcuff people into paying for something they hate.
- Optional: "cancellation fee for a cause" — donate the fee to a cause the customer opposes, adding a second reason to stay.

**Origin.** A high-ticket salesman who wanted to work little and earned a few million a year with no employees, collecting monthly payments with almost no churn.

**When to use.** When a traditional contract makes sense; long-term, high-ticket, slow-to-show-results services.
