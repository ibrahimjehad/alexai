CLOSER is Hormozi's basic one-on-one sales script: Clarify, Label, Overview, Sell the vacation, Explain away concerns, Reinforce.

**The six steps.**
1. **C - Clarify why they're here.** "What made you hop on the call / reply / comment / opt in?" Almost every prospect (except a true first-pickup cold call) took an action toward you; asking why puts the frame in your favour, since they came to you and you are just receiving.
2. **L - Label them with a problem.** "So what I'm hearing is you want [outcome] and you haven't got it yet. Does that sound about right?" Get a yes.
3. **O - Overview past experiences.** "What have you done so far to make this happen? Why is it so important? What has not having it cost you?" This is the pain cycle ([sales-pain-cycle]); it temporarily raises how much they want the outcome. In Ep 953 he adds closing every alternative here ([sales-close-the-other-doors]).
4. **S - Sell the vacation.** Talk about the destination, not the plane flight ([sales-sell-the-vacation]), usually as a three-point pitch ([sales-three-pillar-pitch]) that ends with "If you had all three, would you succeed?" Then ask for the sale.
5. **E - Explain away concerns.** If they don't say yes, handle whichever of the five obstacle types comes up ([sales-five-objection-categories]), using AAA between asks ([sales-aaa-framework]).
6. **R - Reinforce the decision.** Once they buy, the work begins: wow them in the first 24 hours ([sales-reinforce-the-decision]).

**Levels.** Hormozi calls CLOSER "sales 101", AAA objection handling "sales 201", and frame congruence ([sales-frame-congruence-what-else-would-have-to-be-true]) "sales 301".

**Why it matters.** It is simple enough to hand to someone else. After selling every gym membership himself for over a year, Hormozi watched a new hire close a sale using CLOSER and realised the business could run without him. A shared script also makes coaching possible (see [sales-script-memorization-blackout]).

**Ep 305 adds (the earlier, longer version).** Hormozi distilled CLOSER from hundreds of scripts: wording varied, but hitting these milestones closed the sale, for B2C and B2B, from $500 to $100,000 tickets. Details he gives there:
- *Clarify:* if they say "I just want more information", ask what the information was meant to solve. Don't ask for the sale until they've stated why they need a solution ([sales-dont-pitch-until-you-know-yes]).
- *Label:* they have to say it and own it: "I can't cure cancer until you admit you have it."
- *Overview:* repeat the pain cycle until you've exhausted every past attempt (ammo for the close), then explain it wasn't their fault: each attempt lacked a piece you provide ([sales-missing-piece-not-your-fault]).
- *Sell the vacation:* three pillars with short stories, no "technobabble", no longer than about three minutes. On the whole call you only say questions, restatements and stories ([sales-three-things-to-say]).
- *Reinforce:* the R was added years later, once he saw that the sale continues through the whole relationship: you re-close them at onboarding, at the first check-in, and whenever they must act instead of "watching Netflix".
He frames it as framework one of three (closing, conviction, scaling a team): learning to sell is one skill, teaching others to sell is a more valuable one.

**Ep 112 adds.** For teams, CLOSER can be compressed further into two yes/no questions: is it worth it, and do you believe we can deliver? ([sales-two-questions-of-persuasion]).

**When to use.** Any one-on-one sale, phone or in person, especially as the first script for a founder or a new rep. For a done-for-you service, swap the light three-pillar pitch for a detailed pitch ([sales-pitch-style-by-delivery-model]).
