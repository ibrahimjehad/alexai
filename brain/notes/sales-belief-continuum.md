Belief is not yes/no but a scale, and because sales is a transfer of belief, how much the seller believes multiplies everything else.

**The equations.**
- **Close rate = sales skill x belief.** Skill runs roughly 1 to 10; belief can run 1 to 100. A rep with skill 2 and belief 100 will close, because prospects can sense whether you believe it will solve their problem.
- **Units sold = close rate x work ethic.** High activity (faster follow-up, more follow-ups, self-generated opportunities) makes up for a lower close rate.
- **Belief counts twice.** It raises close rate and it drives work ethic: if you truly believe it helps, you follow up faster and more often, as if trying to help someone.

**Why.** You can't pour belief from a cup that's a third full into an empty one and expect it to cross the buying threshold. Belief also depends on knowing the prospect: you can't be convinced you solve a problem you never heard ([sales-train-on-the-prospect-not-the-product]).

**Thought experiment.** Go back five years and tell your past self (without revealing who you are) to put everything into something that later exploded. Your past self says "I'm good, thanks." How many times would you follow up? How certain would you sound? Would you think about technique at all? That's what full belief feels like.

**Test your level.** "Would I bet $1,000 on this? $10,000? Would I sell it to my mother?" (Ep 305.) Missionaries and religious movements sell a new world view with little skill training, because their people believe completely.

**When to use.** Managing a sales team: spend more time raising belief ([sales-build-conviction]) than drilling technique once the basics are in place. If reps don't "drink the Kool-Aid", that is the problem to fix first.
