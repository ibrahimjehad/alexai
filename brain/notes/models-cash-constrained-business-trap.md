When it costs more to get a customer than the first purchase earns, the business starves for cash even if lifetime profit is high.

**The death spiral.** Spend on ads, realize at month end you spent more than you made, cut ads, get fewer customers than you can serve, stop advertising, float the business on personal cash, loans or credit, sell equity to keep the lights on, wait months or years to break even, fall behind, and eventually lose it all.

**Why it happens.** In traditional businesses, a slow drip of profit from many existing customers pays for each new one. Only companies with lots of customers already, or investors' money to burn, can grow that way.

**The two options.** Spending $100 to make $500 profit is a great trade, unless the $500 takes two years to arrive. Then you either (1) wait and pray you do not run out of cash, or (2) get paid fast and grow as much as you want. A good money model is option 2.

**When to use.** When a client's unit economics look fine on paper but they cannot afford to advertise; shift the conversation from LTGP to cash collected in the first 30 days.
